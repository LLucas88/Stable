# ⚙️ Product Operations (Product Ops)

[← Back to all domains](../README.md)

Skills for designing operating cadences, managing tooling and documentation, aligning cross-functional teams, and automating product workflows.

Product Ops is the operating system for product teams. When planning cycles are chaotic, documentation is scattered, and cross-team communication breaks down — it's not a people problem, it's a systems problem. This domain gives your agent the frameworks to design the rhythms, processes, and infrastructure that keep product teams running smoothly.

## 🛠️ Skills in this domain

### 1. `design-operating-cadence`

Use this when a team has no consistent rhythm, meetings are poorly structured, or planning and review cycles are misaligned with delivery.

What it does:
- Identifies all recurring team needs: planning, execution review, retros, stakeholder updates
- Designs the cadence to match team size and velocity
- Balances structure with room for unplanned work

---

### 2. `design-planning-process`

Use this when planning cycles are chaotic, disconnected from strategy, or produce roadmaps that change without rationale.

What it does:
- Creates a repeatable cycle connecting strategy → goals → roadmap → execution
- Defines decision rights at each level
- Includes mid-cycle review points, not just end-of-cycle

---

### 3. `align-cross-team-communication`

Use this when teams work in silos and discover misalignment late, or stakeholders are surprised by product decisions.

What it does:
- Maps all cross-team communication flows: who needs what, how often, in what format
- Reduces noise, increases signal
- Designs for async-first with strong written norms

---

### 4. `run-cross-functional-review`

Use this when a decision requires input from multiple functions, or reviews produce sign-off without genuine alignment.

What it does:
- Defines what is being reviewed and what decision needs to be made
- Requires pre-reads (no pre-read = uninformed input)
- Tests for real engagement, not just "no objections"

---

### 5. `audit-product-process`

Use this when the team feels consistently behind without clear cause, or a scaling team's processes are breaking under load.

What it does:
- Surfaces bottlenecks, waste, and misalignment across product processes
- Focuses on systems, not individuals
- Distinguishes process problems from people or strategy problems

---

### 6. `automate-workflow-governance`

Use this when recurring operations consume too much manual time, or governance standards aren't being enforced.

What it does:
- Maps recurring workflows and estimates time investment
- Identifies automation candidates: repetitive, rule-based, high-frequency
- Designs governance standards with accountability, not just documentation

---

### 7. `manage-tooling-documentation`

Use this when tools are fragmented and creating knowledge silos, or documentation is outdated or unused.

What it does:
- Audits tooling across planning, docs, communication, analytics, roadmapping
- Identifies tool sprawl and consolidation opportunities
- Designs documentation for findability and maintenance, not just creation

---

### 8. `build-product-wiki`

Use this when new team members ramp up too slowly, or decisions get relitigated because rationale was never recorded.

What it does:
- Structures the wiki around how teams work, not how information is created
- Assigns owners to every section (ownerless content goes stale)
- Documents what is repeatedly needed, not everything

## 🧭 How to use this domain

### Typical paths:

**Setting up a new team's operating model:**
1. `design-operating-cadence` → the rhythm
2. `design-planning-process` → how strategy becomes action
3. `align-cross-team-communication` → how teams stay in sync
4. `manage-tooling-documentation` → the infrastructure

**Fixing broken processes:**
1. `audit-product-process` → find the bottlenecks
2. `automate-workflow-governance` → fix what can be automated
3. `design-operating-cadence` → redesign the rhythm

**Building institutional knowledge:**
1. `build-product-wiki` → the knowledge base
2. `manage-tooling-documentation` → the tool stack

**Enter in the middle:**
- Team feels overloaded → start with `audit-product-process`
- Communication silos → start with `align-cross-team-communication`
- Knowledge is in people's heads → start with `build-product-wiki`
- Too many meetings → start with `design-operating-cadence`

---

## ⚠️ What this domain protects against

- **More meetings = more alignment.** It doesn't. `design-operating-cadence` designs for quality over quantity.
- **Automating broken processes.** Faster broken outputs are still broken. `audit-product-process` fixes processes before `automate-workflow-governance` automates them.
- **Tool sprawl as process solution.** New tools don't fix unclear processes — they mask them. `manage-tooling-documentation` fixes process first, then tools.
- **Wiki nobody reads.** Documentation designed for creation, not consumption, becomes digital waste. `build-product-wiki` structures for findability.
- **Reviews as rubber stamps.** "Everyone's aligned" when nobody raised concerns means nobody was engaged. `run-cross-functional-review` tests for real engagement.
