# Align Cross-Team Communication

## Purpose
Help teams design effective communication systems that maintain alignment across product, engineering, design, and business stakeholders without creating overhead.

## Skill type
Conceptual skill

## Use this skill when
- Teams are working in silos and discovering misalignment late
- Engineering builds the wrong thing because product communication is unclear
- Stakeholders are surprised by product decisions or status
- A new cross-functional team needs communication norms established

## Do not use this skill when
- The goal is executive communication and narrative (use build-executive-narrative)
- The goal is operating cadence design (use design-operating-cadence)

## Required inputs
- Teams involved in cross-functional work
- Current communication pain points or failure modes

## Optional inputs
- Current communication channels and tools
- Stakeholder types and needs
- Meeting cadence

## Upstream context
Works best when:
- Operating cadence is designed
- Team structure is clear

## Downstream handoff
Output can feed:
- design-team-rituals
- manage-tooling-documentation

## Instructions
1. Map all cross-team communication flows: who needs what information, how often, in what format.
2. Identify current breakdown points (where misalignment happens most).
3. Design synchronous and async communication channels for each flow.
4. Define norms for decision communication: who is informed, consulted, and accountable.
5. Design status update and escalation protocols.
6. Define feedback loops back to product from engineering and design.

## Output
Provide:
- Communication flow map (who needs what, when, how)
- Current breakdown points
- Recommended communication channels by type
- Decision communication norms (RACI or similar)
- Status update and escalation protocol
- Feedback loop design from engineering/design to product

## Risks / caveats
- More communication channels is not better alignment — reduce noise, increase signal
- Decisions that aren't communicated still need to be justified — design for transparency
- Async-first works well but requires strong written communication norms
