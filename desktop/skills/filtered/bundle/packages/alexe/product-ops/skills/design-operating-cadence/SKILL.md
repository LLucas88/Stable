# Design Operating Cadence

## Purpose
Help teams design a structured operating cadence that keeps planning, execution, and alignment running smoothly across product, engineering, and design.

## Skill type
Conceptual skill

## Use this skill when
- A team has no consistent operating rhythm
- Meetings are too many, too few, or poorly structured
- Planning and review cycles are misaligned with delivery
- A new team or function needs an operating model

## Do not use this skill when
- The goal is a single meeting agenda or facilitation (out of scope)
- The goal is project-level delivery planning (use plan-delivery-collaboration)

## Required inputs
- Team structure (product, engineering, design, leadership involved)
- Current state of the operating process (even rough description)

## Optional inputs
- Team size
- Current pain points
- Planning horizon (quarterly, 6-week cycles, etc.)
- Stakeholder reporting requirements

## Upstream context
Works best when:
- Goals and OKRs are defined
- Team structure is known

## Downstream handoff
Output can feed:
- align-cross-team-communication
- manage-tooling-documentation

## Instructions
1. Identify all recurring team needs: planning, execution review, retrospectives, stakeholder updates.
2. Design the meeting cadence: frequency, duration, attendees, goal for each.
3. Define the planning cycle: how often, what outputs, who is involved.
4. Define the review and reporting cadence.
5. Identify where decisions are made and who makes them.
6. Design the async communication norms to complement meetings.

## Output
Provide:
- Meeting cadence design (recurring meetings with frequency, duration, goal, attendees)
- Planning cycle design
- Review and reporting schedule
- Decision-making framework (who decides what)
- Async communication norms
- Known failure modes and mitigations

## Risks / caveats
- More meetings is not more alignment — quality over quantity
- Cadence design must match team size and velocity
- Leave room for unplanned work — don't over-schedule teams
