## Input
I'm a new Head of Product joining a 6-person product team (3 PMs, 2 designers, 1 product analyst) at a 150-person B2B SaaS company. I've been here 6 weeks. I want to understand where our operating model is breaking down before I redesign anything.

What I've observed so far:
- Discovery: no formal process. PMs talk to customers individually, but findings rarely get shared. Two PMs told me they ran research on the same customer segment without knowing it.
- Planning: quarterly planning happens but it's driven top-down by the CEO. PMs get a priority list and turn it into a roadmap. No structured process for surfacing insights upward.
- Spec process: some PMs write detailed specs, others do nothing in writing. Engineering leads say they're often handed a Jira ticket with two sentences and expected to estimate.
- Launch process: no standard checklist. One PM has a personal launch template. The others wing it.
- Retrospectives: happen after big releases only, and only for the engineering side. Product doesn't run its own retros.

Cross-functional friction I've heard about:
- Engineering: "Product changes scope mid-sprint without explanation"
- CS: "We find out about launches on the day they go live"
- Sales: "Features ship but we don't know how to talk about them"

## Expected output
Process inventory:
1. Customer discovery and research
2. Quarterly planning
3. Specification and handoff to engineering
4. Launch process
5. Retrospectives

Assessment per process:

Discovery:
- Existence: informal; no documented process
- Consistency: inconsistent — depends on individual PM
- Effectiveness: poor; duplicate research, no shared synthesis, insights don't feed planning
- Issue type: missing process + inconsistent execution

Quarterly planning:
- Existence: exists but poorly designed
- Consistency: happens quarterly
- Effectiveness: low; top-down without structured PM input; no way for PMs to surface research or insights
- Issue type: wrong design

Specification and engineering handoff:
- Existence: some PMs do it, some don't
- Consistency: inconsistent across PMs
- Effectiveness: mixed; engineering can't reliably estimate from what they receive
- Issue type: inconsistent execution + missing standards

Launch process:
- Existence: one PM has a template; no team-wide process
- Consistency: inconsistent
- Effectiveness: poor; CS and sales are surprised by launches
- Issue type: missing process

Retrospectives:
- Existence: engineering runs post-release retros; product does not
- Consistency: ad-hoc
- Effectiveness: learning doesn't reach the product team
- Issue type: missing process for product; insufficient scope for what exists

Cross-functional friction points:
- Engineering: scope changes without communication are a trust issue, not just a process issue — need explicit scope change protocol
- CS: no launch communication standard; fix is a simple checklist with CS as a mandatory step
- Sales: no enablement loop; product ships without notifying sales

Priority improvement list:

Immediate (can start this sprint):
1. Spec minimum standard: one-page template PMs must complete before handing off to engineering — eliminates 2-sentence tickets. Owner: Head of Product. Effort: 1 week.
2. Launch checklist: adopt and adapt the existing PM's template as the team standard. Add CS and sales as mandatory steps. Owner: PM lead + CS lead. Effort: 1 week.

Structural (require redesign, 1-2 quarters):
3. Discovery process: shared research repository, lightweight synthesis template, findings presented at monthly team session. Requires PM habit change and tooling.
4. Quarterly planning redesign: PM input mechanism before CEO priority-setting. Structured upward reporting of insights and risks. Requires CPO or CEO buy-in.
5. Product retrospectives: monthly, PM-owned, focused on process and decisions — separate from engineering retros.

Quick wins:
- Spec template (immediate, high impact, no stakeholder approval needed)
- Launch checklist update (immediate, fixes CS/sales complaint directly)

Structural changes:
- Discovery process and planning redesign (require socialization and stakeholder alignment before implementation)
