## Input
People are churning early from our meal-kit subscription. We want to understand why. Maybe we should talk to some of them?

## Expected output
Study goal:
Understand why subscribers are cancelling early and what might retain them.

Research questions (provisional):
1. What drove subscribers to sign up, and where did the experience fall short?
2. What was the tipping point that led to cancellation?
3. What would have kept them subscribed?

Method selection:
Semi-structured interviews (recommended)

Rationale: "Why" questions about behavior and motivation are best answered through conversation, not surveys. Interviews allow follow-up on individual stories.

Study protocol (draft — needs refinement):
- Duration: 30–45 minutes per session
- Format: Remote video call
- Structure: sign-up motivation → early experience → what changed → cancellation decision → what would have helped

Participant profile (needs confirmation):
- Subscribers who cancelled within a specific window (define "early" — first 1–3 months?)
- Sample: 6–8 participants
- Screening: exclude cancellations due to non-product reasons (relocation, etc.)

Missing information:
- What "early" means — after how many deliveries or months?
- Churn rate and whether it's getting worse
- Whether a cancellation survey exists (could inform interview questions)
- Budget and timeline for the study
- Whether the team has run user research before (affects logistics planning)

Data capture plan:
To be defined — recommend video recording plus structured note-taking.

Analysis approach:
Affinity mapping by theme. Look for patterns across at least 3 participants before treating something as a finding.

Suggested next steps:
1. Define what "early churn" means in your product (time or delivery count)
2. Check if cancellation survey data exists
3. Confirm budget, timeline, and who will moderate
4. Then finalize the protocol

Confidence level:
Provisional — study design is directionally sound but needs specifics to be actionable.
