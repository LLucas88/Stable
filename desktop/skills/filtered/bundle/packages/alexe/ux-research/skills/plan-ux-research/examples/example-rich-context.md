## Input
We run Draftboard, a fantasy sports platform that's currently US-only (NFL, NBA, MLB). We're expanding to European markets starting with the UK and Germany, targeting European football (soccer). We need to research how European users think about fantasy sports — their mental models, expectations, and what would make them try a new platform.

Existing US research (from last year):
- US users value real-time scoring and head-to-head leagues
- Social features (trash talk, league chat) are a top retention driver
- 70% of US users play in exactly one league
- Biggest churn driver: losing interest mid-season

Target markets: UK and Germany (initial launch)
Timeline: research must be complete within 6 weeks (launch planned for August, pre-season)
Budget: up to 12 participant incentives ($40 each), plus tooling costs for remote sessions
Team: 1 PM (me), 1 designer, no dedicated researcher

Hypotheses:
- European football fans may prefer season-long formats over weekly contests
- Social dynamics around football in Europe may differ from US sports culture
- Transfer market mechanics (unique to football) may be a key engagement hook

## Expected output
Research question(s):
1. How do European football fans (UK and Germany) currently engage with fantasy sports, predictions, or sports-adjacent games?
2. What mental models do they have about fantasy sports — how do they expect it to work?
3. What features or mechanics would motivate them to try a new fantasy football platform?
4. How do social dynamics around football differ from US sports culture in ways that affect product design?

Selected method: Semi-structured interviews (remote)

Rationale: We're exploring mental models and expectations in an unfamiliar market. Interviews allow us to follow threads we don't anticipate. Surveys would be premature — we don't know enough to write good multiple-choice options yet.

Participant criteria and sample size:
- 12 participants total: 6 UK, 6 Germany
- Must be active European football fans (watch regularly, follow a club)
- Mix of: current fantasy sports players (any platform), former players, and never-played
  - Target: 4 current, 4 former, 4 never-played (2 of each per country)
- Age 20–45
- Exclude: professional sports bettors, sports industry employees
- German sessions conducted in English (screen for English fluency) or with a translator

Discussion guide outline:
1. Sports fandom (5 min): Which clubs, how they follow football, social context (watch with friends? online communities?)
2. Fantasy/prediction experience (10 min): Have they played fantasy sports? Which platforms? What did they like/dislike? If never played — what do they know about it? What would make them try?
3. Mental model exploration (10 min): Show them a blank concept ("imagine a fantasy football app") — what do they expect? How would scoring work? How would leagues work? What would make it fun?
4. Feature reactions (10 min): Present 4–5 feature concepts (transfer market, head-to-head, season-long league, social chat, weekly contests) — rank interest, discuss reactions
5. Motivation and barriers (5 min): What would make them download and try it? What would stop them?
6. Wrap-up (5 min): Anything else, referrals to other fans

Timeline:
- Week 1: Finalize discussion guide, set up recruitment
- Week 2: Recruit and screen participants (use panel service for UK/Germany)
- Weeks 3–4: Run 12 sessions (3 per week, ~45 min each)
- Week 5: Synthesize findings
- Week 6: Share findings with team, inform product decisions

Analysis and sharing plan:
- Affinity mapping of quotes and observations by theme
- Compare UK vs. Germany patterns
- Compare current/former/never-played segments
- Deliverable: findings deck with key themes, per-market differences, and product implications
- Share in a 60-min team session with the designer and stakeholders

Open questions or risks:
- Language barrier for German participants — budget may need to include a translator or we limit to English-speaking Germans (introduces selection bias)
- Recruitment: finding "never played fantasy" football fans who are willing to discuss it may require broader outreach
- US research is a year old — should we refresh US benchmarks for comparison?
- The PM (non-researcher) will moderate — recommend a pilot session to practice and a facilitation checklist
