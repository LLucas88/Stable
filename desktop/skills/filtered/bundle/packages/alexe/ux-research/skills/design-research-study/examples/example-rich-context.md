## Input
We run Minted, a subscription meal-kit delivery app. We're seeing high churn after month 2: 40% of subscribers cancel before their third delivery. We ran a cancellation survey (n=312) and the top reasons were:

1. "Too expensive for what I get" (34%)
2. "Recipes got repetitive" (28%)
3. "I didn't have time to cook" (19%)
4. "Delivery scheduling didn't work for me" (12%)
5. Other (7%)

We want to design a research study to understand the deeper motivations behind these reasons and find out what would have kept subscribers engaged.

Target participants: subscribers who cancelled between month 2 and month 4 in the last 90 days. We have access to their email addresses. Budget allows for 8–10 participant incentives ($50 gift cards). Timeline: study design this week, recruitment next week, sessions in 2 weeks.

## Expected output
Study goal:
Understand the underlying motivations and unmet expectations behind early subscriber churn (month 2–4), and identify what product or experience changes could extend subscriber lifetime.

Research questions:
1. What expectations did subscribers have when they signed up, and where did reality diverge?
2. For each cancellation reason (price, repetition, time, delivery), what was the tipping point that led to cancellation?
3. What, if anything, did subscribers try before cancelling (skipping weeks, changing plans)?
4. What would have changed their decision to cancel?

Method selection:
Semi-structured interviews (remote, 45 minutes each)

Rationale: The cancellation survey provides the "what" — interviews are needed to understand the "why" behind each reason. Semi-structured format allows follow-up on individual experiences while maintaining comparability across participants.

Study protocol:
- Duration: 45 minutes per session
- Format: Remote video call
- Structure:
  1. Warm-up (5 min): How they heard about Minted, what made them subscribe
  2. Early experience (10 min): First deliveries, initial impressions vs. expectations
  3. Turning point (15 min): When things changed, what they noticed, what they tried, what pushed them to cancel
  4. Cancellation moment (10 min): The decision itself, what they considered, whether anything could have changed it
  5. Wrap-up (5 min): What they'd tell a friend about Minted, what one thing they'd change

Participant profile and screening criteria:
- Subscribed to Minted for at least 2 months
- Cancelled within the last 90 days
- Cancelled between month 2 and month 4
- Mix of cancellation reasons (recruit 2–3 per top reason to cover price, repetition, time, and delivery)
- Exclude anyone who cancelled due to relocation or dietary change (not actionable)
- Target: 8 participants

Data capture plan:
- Video recording (with consent) for team review
- Live note-taking by a second team member using a structured observation template
- Template columns: topic area, participant quote, emotional tone, insight/pattern

Analysis approach and success criteria:
- Affinity mapping of quotes and observations by theme
- Compare stated cancellation reason (from survey) to deeper motivation (from interview)
- Success: identify 3–5 actionable retention levers with supporting evidence from at least 3 participants each

Pilot plan:
- Run 1 pilot session with an internal team member who recently churned from a similar subscription service
- Test: question flow, timing, recording setup, note-taking template
- Adjust protocol based on pilot findings before recruiting

Known risks and mitigations:
- Recall bias: participants cancelled up to 90 days ago and may not remember details. Mitigation: share their order history before the session to refresh memory.
- Self-serving bias: participants may blame external factors. Mitigation: ask about specific moments and behaviors, not just opinions.
- Recruitment difficulty: churned users may not respond. Mitigation: offer $50 incentive, send personalized email from the founder.

Timeline:
- Week 1: Finalize protocol, run pilot
- Week 2: Recruit and schedule participants
- Weeks 3–4: Run 8 sessions (4 per week)
- Week 4: Synthesize and share findings
