## Input
We run a B2C productivity app — to-do and task management, freemium, mobile-first. Here's what we have on growth:
- Acquisition: mostly organic App Store (40%), Google Ads (35%), word of mouth (25%)
- Activation: 31% of installs complete onboarding and create their first task list within 24h
- Retention: Day-7 is 28%, Day-30 is 16%
- Referral: we have a "share with friend" feature but it generates maybe 3–4% of new installs
- Revenue: 4.8% free-to-paid conversion, $5.99/month or $39.99/year. MRR is ~$48k

The team has been focused on acquisition for 18 months. Growth has flatlined — adding users but churn is eating the gains. We're not sure where to focus.

## Expected output
Growth funnel map with metrics at each stage:

| Stage | Metric | Current |
|---|---|---|
| Acquisition | New installs/month | Unknown volume, mix: 40% organic / 35% paid / 25% WOM |
| Activation | 24h task list creation rate | 31% of installs |
| Retention | Day-7 / Day-30 | 28% / 16% |
| Referral | Referral-driven installs | ~3–4% of new installs |
| Revenue | Free-to-paid conversion | 4.8% |

Primary growth loop description:
Currently: paid acquisition loop (spend money → get installs → some convert to paid → fund more spend). This loop doesn't compound — it requires continuous spend to maintain.
Weak secondary loop: word of mouth (25% of installs), but no mechanism to accelerate it. The "share with friend" feature isn't creating a reinforcing cycle — it's passive.

Stage-level assessment:
- Acquisition: functioning but expensive and linear. 35% paid dependency creates fragility.
- Activation: underperforming. 31% is low for a task management app — the first session isn't delivering value fast enough. This is the biggest gap.
- Retention: concerning. Day-30 at 16% means most users who activate still churn. At this retention level, the product hasn't established a habit.
- Referral: weak / missing. 3-4% with a share feature suggests the share mechanism is passive, not compounding. No incentive structure, no natural sharing moment.
- Revenue: 4.8% conversion is workable if retention were stronger, but a leaky retention model makes revenue growth slow.

Leverage analysis:
Improving activation from 31% to 40% (+9pp) would increase the pool of retained users by ~29% without increasing acquisition spend. Given Day-30 retention of 16%, every extra activated user stays ~16% of the time. This cascades into revenue.

Improving Day-30 retention from 16% to 22% would increase LTV by ~37.5% per cohort — and reduce the acquisition spend needed to maintain revenue targets.

Fixing acquisition volume while retention is at 16% is filling a leaking bucket. The 18 months of acquisition focus with flatlined growth is the evidence: more input isn't solving the output problem.

Recommended growth priorities:
1. Activation (immediate): redesign first-session experience to ensure users create and complete a task, not just install. 31% → 40%+ is achievable without engineering-heavy work.
2. Retention (quarter): identify the Day-7 drop-off cause. 28% Day-7 is reasonable; the fall from Day-7 to Day-30 (28% → 16%) is where the habit isn't forming. Investigate what Day-7 retained users do that Day-30 churned users don't.
3. Referral loop (next quarter): add a compelling sharing moment — "share a task list with a collaborator" creates a natural product-led loop where sharing the output of the product is also the acquisition mechanic.

Data gaps:
- No activation funnel breakdown by step (which step in onboarding loses users?)
- No Day-7 vs. Day-30 behavioral difference data (what do retained users do differently?)
- CAC by channel unknown (is the 35% Google Ads spend economically sound given 16% Day-30 retention?)
