## Input
Investment: building an in-app referral program. Engineering estimate: 6 weeks of 2 engineers + 1 designer = $28,000 in fully-loaded team cost. Ongoing maintenance: $2,000/year.
Expected benefit: increase referral-driven new user acquisition from current 8% of new users to 18% of new users. Current new user volume: 1,200/month. Average LTV of referred users: $95 (based on historical cohort data — referred users retain 22% better than paid).
Current paid acquisition cost: $22 CAC blended.
Timeline to value: referral program takes ~2 months to reach full adoption after launch.

## Expected output
Investment definition and cost:
- One-time development cost: $28,000
- Ongoing annual maintenance: $2,000
- Timeline: 6 weeks development, 2 months to full adoption

Expected return and how it's quantified:
The referral program shifts 10 percentage points of monthly acquisition from paid ($22 CAC) to referral (assumed cost $4/referral reward + program overhead ≈ $6 effective CAC).
At 1,200 new users/month and 10pp referral share increase: 120 additional referred users/month instead of paid.

Monthly cost saving: 120 users × ($22 paid CAC − $6 referral CAC) = 120 × $16 = $1,920/month
Annual cost saving: $1,920 × 12 = $23,040

Additionally, referred users have higher LTV ($95 vs. ~$70 average for paid users). Annual LTV uplift from cohort quality: 120 users/month × $25 LTV difference × 12 months = $36,000/year in incremental gross profit from cohort quality improvement.

ROI calculation (range: conservative / base / optimistic):

Conservative (8% referral share achieved, not 18%):
- Incremental referral users: 0 net new (already at 8%) — only cohort quality applies to existing referral base
- Wait, this means the program doesn't increase referral from 8% to 18% — only the 10pp uplift is the incremental return
- Revised conservative: 5pp uplift (60 users/month vs. 120)
- Annual saving: 60 × $16 × 12 = $11,520 + LTV uplift $18,000 = $29,520/year

Base case (10pp uplift, 120 users/month shifted to referral):
- Annual saving: $23,040 + LTV uplift $36,000 = $59,040/year
- ROI (year 1): ($59,040 − $28,000 − $2,000) / $28,000 = $29,040 / $28,000 = 104%

Optimistic (12pp uplift due to viral amplification):
- Annual saving: $27,648 + LTV uplift $43,200 = $70,848/year
- ROI (year 1): ($70,848 − $30,000) / $28,000 = 145%

Key assumptions driving the return:
1. Referral share increases from 8% to 18% — this is the central assumption. If the program underperforms and only reaches 11-12%, return is lower but still positive.
2. Referral users retain 22% better — based on historical cohort data. Confidence: high.
3. Referral effective CAC of $6 — includes $4 reward + program overhead. If rewards are higher (e.g., $10), effective CAC rises and the saving shrinks.

Payback period or break-even timeline:
Base case: $59,040/year in annual benefit. Monthly benefit: $4,920. Time to recover $28,000 investment: $28,000 / $4,920 = 5.7 months. Break-even reached before end of year 1 (accounting for 2-month ramp-up: month 8 from project start).

Conservative: $29,520/year. Monthly benefit: $2,460. Payback: ~11 months from launch.

Top uncertainties and how to reduce them:
1. Referral uptake rate: the 10pp increase is the biggest assumption and hasn't been tested. Mitigation: soft-launch to 20% of users first, measure referral send rate and conversion before full rollout.
2. Reward cost: if viral coefficients require higher rewards to work, effective CAC rises. Mitigation: model at $8 and $12 reward levels.

Recommendation: invest
Even under conservative assumptions (5pp uplift), the investment pays back within 12 months and produces positive ROI. The higher-quality cohort effect from referred users adds value beyond the CAC saving. Investment is justified; validate referral uptake with a limited pilot before scaling the reward program.
