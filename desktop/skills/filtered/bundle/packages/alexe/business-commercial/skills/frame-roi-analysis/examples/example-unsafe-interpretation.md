## Input
Calculate the ROI of improving our search feature. This will make users happier and increase engagement. Assume the improvement generates $500,000 in value.

## Expected output
Status:
The ROI cannot be calculated using an assumed value that isn't derived from measurable business outcomes.

Why "$500,000 in value" is not a valid input:
ROI requires benefit that is traceable to measurable business outcomes: revenue increase, churn reduction, CAC reduction, or cost savings. "Makes users happier" and "increases engagement" are not business outcomes unless they connect to a specific metric that moves revenue or reduces costs.

If the improvement increases retention by X%, that can be translated to LTV improvement.
If it reduces churn by X%, that can be translated to incremental ARR retained.
If it reduces support volume, that has a cost saving.

The problem with assuming the value upfront:
If the team assumes $500,000 in value and then calculates ROI against it, the ROI simply reflects the assumption — not a real business analysis. The number becomes circular and misleading.

What the ROI framing should do instead:
1. Define what business metric the search improvement is expected to move (retention, conversion, engagement that drives upsell)
2. Quantify the current baseline for that metric
3. Estimate a realistic improvement (supported by user research or comparable product data)
4. Calculate the value from the improvement using LTV or ARR math

Example of a valid framing:
"Search improvements for SaaS tools typically increase user task completion rate. If task completion is correlated with our Day-30 retention (hypothesis), a 2pp improvement in retention for 500 new users/month at $85 ARPU and 70% margin represents an incremental LTV gain of ~$60,000/year."

This is still an estimate with uncertainty — but it's derived from business logic, not an assumed value.

What this skill produces:
A framework for estimating the ROI from search improvements, requiring the team to specify which metric they expect to move and by how much. The value flows from the metric impact, not the other way around.
