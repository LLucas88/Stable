## Input
We have a SaaS product priced at $29/month per user. Growth is slowing. We're thinking about switching to usage-based pricing. Is that a good idea?

## Expected output
Current model description:
Per-user subscription at $29/month. No information on current NRR, churn, or customer feedback on pricing.

Value metric assessment:
The right value metric for pricing depends on what drives value in this product. Per-user pricing works well when: (a) value scales clearly with headcount, and (b) users don't share access or avoid adding seats. Usage-based pricing works better when value scales with activity (volume, transactions, queries), not headcount.

Without knowing what the product does and how customers use it, it's not possible to say whether usage-based pricing is appropriate.

Expansion revenue analysis:
Unknown — NRR not provided. If growth is slowing, the question is whether it's slowing at the acquisition stage (new customers aren't converting) or the expansion stage (existing customers aren't growing). These require different responses. Usage-based pricing primarily helps the expansion problem, not the acquisition problem.

Structural issues (conditional):
If the product has the following patterns, usage-based may make sense:
- Login sharing is happening (customers avoiding seat costs)
- NRR is below 100% (expansion is a problem)
- Customers use the product at highly variable volumes (some use it 100x more than others)

If the product doesn't have these patterns, usage-based pricing introduces revenue unpredictability without solving the actual growth problem.

Alternative model options:
- Usage-based: good if activity-based value delivery; introduces billing complexity and revenue variance
- Tiered flat fee (not per-seat): reduces expansion friction without full usage-based complexity
- Keep per-seat with better onboarding: if low adoption (not pricing) is the growth problem, pricing changes won't fix it

Recommendation: investigate before changing
Before changing the pricing model, answer:
1. What is the current NRR? (below 100% suggests expansion problem; above 100% suggests pricing model is working)
2. Why is growth slowing? (acquisition-side or expansion-side?)
3. Are customers complaining about pricing? (CS and churn interview data)
4. What do power users pay vs. casual users? (if variance is high, usage-based has merit)

Missing information:
- Current NRR and churn data
- Customer feedback on pricing
- Usage variance across customer accounts

Confidence level:
Context-informed
