## Input
Product and engineering don't collaborate well at our company. Engineers feel left out. We want to improve it.

## Expected output
Collaboration health assessment (provisional):

"Engineers feel left out" is a signal that maps to one of three common failure modes:
1. Engineers are not involved in discovery or scoping — they find out what they're building when the spec arrives
2. Engineers are involved but their input is ignored or overridden without explanation
3. Engineers don't understand the context (why something is being built) even when they're technically included

These have different fixes. Without knowing which one applies, the recommendation would be too generic.

Involvement design per phase:

PM-engineering decision interface:
Not defined — key question: what decisions can engineers make, and what is reserved for PM? If this isn't explicit, engineers will feel like they have input but no influence.

Estimation process:
Not assessed — is estimation a problem? "Feel left out" doesn't necessarily mean estimation is broken.

Engineering-to-product feedback loop:
Not designed. This is usually missing in teams where engineers feel excluded — they have no structured channel to raise concerns.

Suggested quick diagnostics:
- Ask three engineers: "At what point in the process do you first find out what you'll be building?" The answer reveals the entry point.
- Ask: "Has your technical recommendation ever changed a product decision?" If the answer is no or rarely, inclusion is symbolic.

- What specifically engineers feel left out of (discovery? prioritization? design?)
- Whether engineers are involved in any phase currently
