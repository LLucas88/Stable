# 🧭 Product Strategy

[← Back to all domains](../README.md)

Skills for setting long-term product direction, assessing market fit, analyzing competitive landscape, defining positioning, and making portfolio-level investment decisions.

Strategy is the set of choices that make other choices easier. Without it, every roadmap debate starts from scratch, every stakeholder has a different "north star," and product work becomes opinion-driven. This domain gives your agent the frameworks to articulate where the product is heading, why, and what it deliberately won't do.

- `craft-product-mission` defines what the product does for whom right now → feeds `define-long-term-direction`
- `define-long-term-direction` translates vision/mission into a 2–4 year strategic trajectory → feeds `build-portfolio-roadmap-strategy`

**Competitive & positioning cluster:**
- `analyze-competitive-landscape` maps competitors and dynamics → feeds `frame-competitive-differentiation`
- `frame-competitive-differentiation` articulates why this product wins → feeds `define-strategic-positioning`

## 🛠️ Skills in this domain

### 1. `define-product-vision`

Use this when a product lacks a clear vision, or the existing one is vague, outdated, or misaligned with strategy.

What it does:
- Articulates an aspirational end state: what the world looks like if the product succeeds
- Grounds the vision in user outcomes, not features
- Tests for specificity — rejects generic statements that could apply to any product

---

### 2. `craft-product-mission`

Use this when the team confuses vision and mission, or the mission is too vague to guide trade-offs.

What it does:
- Defines what the product does, for whom, and what value it delivers right now
- Ensures the mission is present-tense and operational (not aspirational)
- Tests: if the mission can't reject bad ideas, it's too vague

---

### 3. `define-long-term-direction`

Use this when vision and mission exist but don't translate into strategic direction, or the team debates priorities without a shared trajectory.

What it does:
- Articulates how the product evolves from current mission toward vision
- Identifies what areas will grow, what will be left behind
- Makes trade-offs explicit: what the product will not pursue

---

### 4. `analyze-competitive-landscape`

Use this when a team lacks a structured view of competitors, or the landscape has shifted.

What it does:
- Maps direct, indirect, and status quo competitors
- Assesses how each competes and where the landscape is evolving
- Triangulates with customer feedback, not just public marketing materials

---

### 5. `define-strategic-positioning`

Use this when the product is trying to be everything to everyone, or strategy lacks a clear "who we're for."

What it does:
- Makes deliberate choices about where to play and how to win
- Defines who the product is for and — critically — who it's not for
- Ensures the differentiation point is something the target segment actually values

---

### 6. `frame-competitive-differentiation`

Use this when the team struggles to articulate why their product is different, or sales needs a competitive narrative.

What it does:
- Identifies primary competitive alternatives
- Articulates what differentiates the product with specificity
- Rejects weak differentiation: "better UX" and "more features" aren't enough

---

### 7. `assess-product-market-fit`

Use this when the team is unsure about PMF, or there are mixed signals from retention, growth, and feedback.

What it does:
- Evaluates fit signals: retention, engagement, qualitative resonance, growth patterns
- Identifies where fit is strong, weak, or unknown
- Refuses to declare PMF from a single metric

---

### 8. `build-portfolio-roadmap-strategy`

Use this when a team needs to prioritize across multiple product areas, or leadership needs investment trade-off decisions.

What it does:
- Maps all current product areas, bets, and investment themes
- Evaluates each against strategic goals and PMF signals
- Structures the portfolio with explicit trade-offs and deprioritizations visible

---

### 9. `prioritize-strategic-bets`

Use this when multiple large initiatives compete for resources, or the team is unsure which bets align with strategy.

What it does:
- Evaluates each bet on: goal, hypothesis, investment required, potential payoff
- Separates core investments from exploratory bets
- Defines kill criteria for explore bets (prevents zombie projects)

## 🧭 How to use this domain

### Typical paths:

**Setting product direction:**
1. `define-product-vision` → aspirational end state
2. `craft-product-mission` → operational "what we do today"
3. `define-long-term-direction` → strategic trajectory

**Understanding the competitive field:**
1. `analyze-competitive-landscape` → who we compete with
2. `frame-competitive-differentiation` → why we win
3. `define-strategic-positioning` → where we play

**Making investment decisions:**
1. `assess-product-market-fit` → where we have fit
2. `build-portfolio-roadmap-strategy` → how to allocate
3. `prioritize-strategic-bets` → what to fund

**Enter in the middle:**
- New competitor appeared → start with `analyze-competitive-landscape`
- Unsure about PMF → start with `assess-product-market-fit`
- Annual planning → start with `build-portfolio-roadmap-strategy`
- Team debates priorities → start with `define-long-term-direction`

## ⚠️ What this domain protects against

- **Vision without trade-offs.** "We'll be the best platform for everyone" is not a strategy. `define-long-term-direction` requires identifying what the product will not pursue.
- **Positioning for everyone.** Which means positioning for no one. `define-strategic-positioning` forces a choice of audience.
- **PMF declared from vibes.** High signup numbers don't mean product-market fit. `assess-product-market-fit` requires retention, engagement, and qualitative resonance.
- **Portfolio without priorities.** Doing everything means finishing nothing. `prioritize-strategic-bets` defines kill criteria for explore bets.
- **Weak differentiation.** "Better UX" isn't a differentiator. `frame-competitive-differentiation` demands specificity anchored in what the target segment values.
