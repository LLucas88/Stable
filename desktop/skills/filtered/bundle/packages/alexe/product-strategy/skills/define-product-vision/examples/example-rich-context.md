# Example: Rich Context

## Input

**Product area:** B2B project management SaaS for distributed engineering teams
**Target segment:** Engineering managers at companies with 50–500 engineers
**Business goal:** Become the default planning layer for async engineering work by 2028
**Key user problem:** Teams lose alignment when work is distributed across time zones; status updates are manual and inconsistent
**Existing vision draft:** "Make engineering work visible"

## Output

### Vision statement
A world where every engineering team — regardless of size or location — maintains perfect clarity on what's being built, why it matters, and what's at risk.

### Mission statement
We give distributed engineering teams a single source of truth for planning and progress, so managers spend less time chasing updates and more time enabling their teams.

### Target segment
Engineering managers at mid-size tech companies (50–500 engineers) with distributed or async-first teams.

### Core user problem
Status is invisible by default. Managers must manually gather information across tools and people, which breaks down at scale and across time zones.

### Strategic time horizon
3–5 years (becoming the default async planning layer for engineering orgs)

### Confidence level
Context-informed

### Open questions
- What does "default planning layer" mean operationally — does it replace existing tools or integrate?
- Is the segment limited to software engineering or broader R&D?
