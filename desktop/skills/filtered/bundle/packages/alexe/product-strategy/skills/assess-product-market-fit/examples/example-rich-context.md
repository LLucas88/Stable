# Example: Rich Context

## Input

**Product:** B2B analytics platform for e-commerce operations teams
**Segment:** Operations leads at e-commerce companies with $10M–$200M revenue
**Signals available:**
- 6-month retention: 78% (paid accounts)
- NPS: +42
- Top 3 churn reasons: "too complex", "team didn't adopt it", "switched to internal tools"
- Qualitative feedback from 12 user interviews: strong love from power users, low adoption outside the core analytics persona
- MoM growth: 8% new accounts, but 4% expansion (low upsell)

## Output

### Fit assessment level
**Early signals** — strong retention and NPS among power users, but adoption and expansion gaps suggest fit is persona-specific, not team-wide.

### Key signals

| Signal | Strength | Notes |
|--------|----------|-------|
| 6-month retention (78%) | Strong | Above typical SaaS benchmarks for this segment |
| NPS +42 | Strong | High promoter score suggests real value for core users |
| Low upsell (4% expansion) | Weak | Indicates value is siloed to one persona, not spreading |
| Churn reason: complexity | Risk | Points to onboarding and UX gaps for non-analysts |
| Interview resonance | Mixed | Deep love from analysts, low interest from ops leads |

### Gaps and risks
1. Fit is confirmed for analytics power users but not for the stated segment (ops leads)
2. Low team-wide adoption blocks expansion revenue
3. "Too complex" churn reason is an adoption blocker at scale

### Recommended next steps
1. Narrow ICP to analytics-heavy teams rather than general ops leads
2. Run usability research on non-analyst persona to understand adoption barriers
3. Test a simplified onboarding flow to improve team-level activation
4. Re-evaluate product positioning — are you selling to the right buyer?

### Confidence level
Context-informed — signals are real but interpretation requires validation with more segments.
