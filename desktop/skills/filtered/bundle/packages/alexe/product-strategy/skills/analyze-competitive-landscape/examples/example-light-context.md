# Example: Light Context

## Input

**Product:** Online course platform for professional skills
**Target segment:** Working professionals who want to upskill
**Known competitors:** Coursera, Udemy, LinkedIn Learning

## Output

### Competitor inventory (partial)

**Direct competitors named:**
- Coursera — university-partnered courses, certificates, degrees
- Udemy — marketplace model, broad topic coverage, individual instructors
- LinkedIn Learning — subscription library, integrated with LinkedIn career graph

**Likely direct competitors not mentioned:**
- Skillshare — creative and professional skills, subscription
- Pluralsight — technical/developer skills, B2B and individual
- Maven — cohort-based courses, peer learning

**Indirect competitors:**
- YouTube (free tutorials)
- Company L&D programs
- Bootcamps (General Assembly, Flatiron)

**Status quo alternative:**
- On-the-job learning and self-directed reading/podcasts

### Competitive dimension map (provisional)

| Dimension | Context needed | Why it matters |
|-----------|---------------|----------------|
| Content quality and depth | What type of professional skills? | Coursera wins on credentials; Udemy on breadth |
| Format (video, cohort, project-based) | Unknown for this product | Format is increasingly a differentiator |
| Price | Unknown | Marketplace (Udemy) vs. subscription (LinkedIn Learning) vs. per-course |
| Career outcome integration | Unknown | LinkedIn Learning has built-in advantage here |
| B2B vs. B2C | Unknown | Changes the entire go-to-market and competitive set |

### What is needed to complete this analysis

1. **What type of professional skills?** (Technical, leadership, creative, domain-specific?) — the competitive set changes entirely
2. **B2B or B2C?** If selling to companies for employee training, Cornerstone and Degreed are key competitors. If direct to individual, the named competitors are correct.
3. **What makes this product different?** Without knowing the differentiation hypothesis, it's impossible to assess where competitive gaps exist
4. **Win/loss data or customer feedback on alternatives** — what do buyers say when they choose a competitor?

### What can be done now
Map the three named competitors on 2–3 key dimensions the team cares about and identify the white space. To do this meaningfully, the team needs to define what dimensions matter for their target customer.

### Confidence level
Low — the landscape is partially identified, but the analysis is directional until segment specificity, competitive dimensions, and differentiation hypothesis are established.
