# Example: Poor Context

## Input

"Can you analyze our competitive landscape?"

## Output

### Status
Insufficient context to produce a competitive analysis.

### What is missing

A competitive landscape analysis requires:
1. **Product area** — what does the product do?
2. **Target segment** — who is the customer?
3. **At least a partial competitor list** — or enough context to identify likely competitors

Without these, any competitor list produced would be guessed and potentially irrelevant.

### Discovery questions

Before analyzing the landscape, answer:

1. What does the product do, and what category does it compete in?
2. Who is the primary customer or user (industry, role, company size)?
3. Who are the competitors the team already knows about or loses deals to?
4. Is this a new analysis or an update to an existing one?
5. What decision will this analysis inform — positioning, roadmap, investment, launch strategy?

### What happens next

Once product area, segment, and at least a partial competitor list are available, this skill can produce:
- Direct, indirect, and status-quo competitor inventory
- Competitor profiles (segment, value prop, capabilities, strategy)
- Competitive dimension map
- Differentiation opportunities and threats

If win/loss data or customer feedback on alternatives is available, include it — it makes the analysis significantly more reliable than public-source-only competitive research.
