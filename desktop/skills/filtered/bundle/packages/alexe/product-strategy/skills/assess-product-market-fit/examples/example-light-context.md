# Example: Light Context

## Input

**Product:** Consumer budgeting app
**Segment:** Millennials trying to save money
**Signals:** "We have decent downloads but people don't really stick around. Some users love it."

## Output

### Fit assessment level
**Pre-fit to early signals** — some qualitative resonance, but retention concerns and vague signals prevent a stronger classification.

### Available signals

| Signal | Strength | Notes |
|--------|----------|-------|
| "Decent downloads" | Unclear | Volume unknown; acquisition ≠ fit |
| Low retention (implied) | Weak | Core fit signal is missing |
| "Some users love it" | Early signal | Indicates potential — who are those users? |

### Gaps and risks
- No retention data provided — this is the most important fit signal and must be collected
- "Millennials trying to save money" is too broad; fit likely varies by sub-segment
- Downloads without activation and retention data cannot indicate fit

### Recommended next steps
1. Measure 30-day and 90-day retention — this is the single most important next step
2. Identify the "some users who love it" — what do they have in common?
3. Run a quick survey: "How disappointed would you be if this product disappeared?" (Sean Ellis test)

### Confidence level
Provisional — data is too thin for a real assessment. Framework above is a starting point only.
