# Example: Light Context

## Input

**Vision:** Be the go-to platform for remote team collaboration.
**Mission:** We help distributed teams stay connected and aligned through async video and messaging tools.
**Business goals:** Grow ARR from $2M to $20M in 3 years.

## Output

### Strategic direction narrative (provisional)
The direction appears to be expanding from a communication tool into a broader async work platform. However, the vision is broad enough to encompass many paths — the direction below is a provisional hypothesis based on available context and should be validated with leadership before informing planning.

### Planning horizon
3 years (assumed based on ARR goal — confirm)

### Trajectory map (provisional)

| Area | Direction | Rationale |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| Async video and messaging | Maintain | Core product; must remain reliable |
| Team rituals and workflows | Grow (assumed) | Likely differentiator vs. Slack/Teams |
| Integrations with work tools | Grow (assumed) | Required to be "platform," not point solution |
| Synchronous collaboration | Unknown | Unclear if this is in or out of scope |

### Defining strategic choices (provisional)
Based on vision and mission, the likely core choice is: async-first as a deliberate design philosophy, not a constraint. But whether this means competing with Loom, Notion, or building something new is unclear.

### Open questions (required before direction can be finalized)
1. Who is the primary target segment — startups, mid-market, enterprise?
2. What does "go-to platform" mean — category leader, preferred tool in a category, or OS for remote work?
3. What is the current retention and engagement profile? (Directional health of the core product)
4. Is the $2M–$20M ARR goal driven by more customers, expansion, or both?
5. What competitive moves have changed the landscape recently (e.g. Loom acquisition by Atlassian)?

### Confidence level
Provisional — vision is directionally useful but too broad to produce a specific strategic direction without segment, competitive context, and product health signals.
