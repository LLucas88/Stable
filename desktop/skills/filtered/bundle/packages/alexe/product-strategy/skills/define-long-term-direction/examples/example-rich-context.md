# Example: Rich Context

## Input

**Vision:** A world where every small business has the same financial clarity as a Fortune 500 company.
**Mission:** We help small business owners understand and control their cash flow through automated bookkeeping and real-time financial dashboards.
**Business goals (3-year horizon):**
- Expand from 50K to 500K paying customers
- Grow ARPU from $45/mo to $120/mo by moving upmarket to teams of 2–20
- Become the financial operating system for small businesses, not just a bookkeeping tool

**Current capabilities:** Automated transaction categorization, cash flow dashboards, invoicing, basic tax prep integrations.
**Key trends:** SMB owners increasingly use AI tools; accounting firms are building software; embedded finance is growing.
**Competitive context:** Legacy players (QuickBooks, Xero) own accountant-mediated workflows; fintech challengers are fragmenting payments and lending.

## Output

### Strategic direction narrative
Over the next 3 years, the product will evolve from a financial visibility tool into the operating layer for small business finances — not just showing what happened, but helping owners act on it. We will expand the user from the solo owner to the small team, and shift from reactive dashboards to proactive financial guidance.

### Planning horizon
3 years (2026–2028)

### Trajectory map

| Area | Direction | Rationale |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| Automated bookkeeping | Maintain | Core foundation; must be reliable but not the growth driver |
| Real-time cash flow dashboards | Grow | High engagement; expand depth and predictive capabilities |
| AI-powered financial guidance | Grow | Key differentiator; move from visibility to advice |
| Team and collaboration features | Grow | Required for upmarket segment (2–20 employees) |
| Tax preparation | Maintain | Table stakes for retention; not a growth area |
| Payments and lending | Explore | Strategic option; dependent on partnership decisions |
| Accountant-mediated workflows | Deprioritize | Legacy players own this; not core to direct SMB motion |

### Defining strategic choices

1. **Direct-to-owner over accountant-mediated.** We build for the business owner making decisions, not the accountant filing taxes. This shapes UX, onboarding, and distribution.

2. **Guidance over reporting.** The next phase of the product shifts from showing data to generating recommendations. We invest in AI-powered insights as a core capability, not a feature add.

3. **Move upmarket deliberately.** The 2–20 employee segment is the primary growth target. Solo-owner features are maintained but not the investment priority.

4. **Platform over point solution.** The direction is toward becoming the financial OS — which means building integrations and a foundation others can build on, not just adding more features.

### Success picture at end of horizon
A small business owner with 5 employees uses the product daily to understand their financial position, receives weekly AI-generated cash flow alerts, and pays bills and manages payroll from within the product. ARPU has doubled, and expansion revenue from team seats is the primary growth driver.

### Key assumptions
- AI-powered guidance is technically feasible within 18 months at production quality
- The 2–20 employee segment has sufficient willingness to pay for the upmarket move
- Direct distribution can scale without requiring an accountant channel

### Open questions
- Do we build payments natively or via embedded finance partnerships?
- What is the migration path for current solo-owner customers as we move upmarket?
- How do we handle accountants who currently refer customers to us?
