# Example: Poor Context

## Input

"We need a long-term direction for our product."

## Output

### Status
Insufficient context to produce a meaningful product direction.

### What is missing

To define a long-term direction, the following are required at minimum:

1. **Vision** — what is the aspirational future state the product is working toward?
2. **Mission** — what does the product do today, for whom, and what value does it deliver?
3. **Business goals** — what does the organization need to achieve in the next 2–4 years?

Without these, any direction produced would be invented rather than derived from strategy.

### Discovery questions

Before producing a direction, establish:

1. What is the product? What does it do and who uses it?
2. Where does the organization want to be in 3–5 years — aspirationally?
3. What is the product doing right now that it must keep doing?
4. What are the most important business outcomes for the next planning cycle?
5. What areas are clearly off the table (things the product will not pursue)?

### What happens next

Once vision, mission, and business goals are available, this skill can produce:
- A strategic direction narrative
- A trajectory map (grow / maintain / deprioritize)
- Defining strategic choices
- Key assumptions and open questions

If vision and mission are not yet defined, use `define-product-vision` and `craft-product-mission` first.
