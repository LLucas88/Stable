# Define Strategic Positioning

## Purpose
Help teams make deliberate choices about where to play and how to win — translating product-market fit insights and competitive context into a positioning that guides strategy, roadmap, and GTM decisions.

## Skill type
Conceptual skill

## Use this skill when
- The product is trying to be everything to everyone and losing focus
- Strategy discussions lack a clear "who we're for and who we're not for"
- A positioning review is needed before a major investment or launch
- Competitive pressure requires sharpening the market position

## Do not use this skill when
- The goal is customer-facing messaging and copy (use develop-positioning-messaging)
- The goal is competitive analysis (use analyze-competitive-landscape)

## Required inputs
- Target segment (or candidate segments)
- Product capabilities and strengths
- Business goals

## Optional inputs
- Product-market fit assessment
- Competitive landscape
- Win/loss data
- ICP (Ideal Customer Profile) definition

## Upstream context
Works best when:
- Vision and mission are defined
- PMF assessment exists
- Competitive landscape is mapped

## If upstream context is missing
Produce a positioning hypothesis clearly labeled as assumed. List what data would validate or refute it.

## Downstream handoff
Output can feed:
- frame-competitive-differentiation (positioning informs differentiation story)
- develop-positioning-messaging (internal positioning → external messaging)
- build-portfolio-roadmap-strategy (positioning constrains portfolio choices)

## Instructions
1. Identify the target segment (be specific — who is this for, and who is it explicitly not for).
2. Identify the frame of reference (what category or alternative does the product compete with?).
3. Identify the point of differentiation (what makes this the right choice for the target segment?).
4. Identify the reason to believe (what evidence supports the differentiation claim?).
5. Draft a positioning statement using: "For [segment], [product] is the [category] that [differentiation] because [reason to believe]."
6. Stress-test: does this positioning exclude the wrong customers and include the right ones?

## Output
Provide:
- Target segment (with explicit exclusions)
- Frame of reference
- Point of differentiation
- Reason to believe
- Positioning statement
- Positioning trade-offs (what this positioning sacrifices)
- Confidence level: provisional / context-informed / validated

## Risks / caveats
- Positioning that tries to include every segment has no real position
- The point of differentiation must be something the target segment actually cares about
- Positioning is a strategic choice, not a description — it requires saying no to things
