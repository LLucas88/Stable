# Frame Competitive Differentiation

## Purpose
Help teams analyze their competitive landscape and articulate a clear, defensible differentiation position.

## Skill type
Conceptual skill

## Use this skill when
- The team struggles to articulate why their product is different or better
- A new competitor has entered the market and positioning needs to be reviewed
- The team is preparing a launch and needs a clear differentiation story
- Sales or marketing needs a competitive narrative

## Do not use this skill when
- The team needs product vision (use define-product-vision)
- The team needs GTM messaging and launch (use gtm skills)
- The goal is pricing and packaging (use pricing skills)

## Required inputs
- Product description and key capabilities
- Target segment
- Known competitors (at least 2–3)

## Optional inputs
- Customer feedback on why they chose your product
- Win/loss analysis
- Product-market fit assessment
- Pricing context
- Positioning statement drafts

## Upstream context
Works best when:
- Product vision is defined
- Target segment is clear
- Key product capabilities are known

## If upstream context is missing
If the competitive landscape is completely unknown or the product is very early-stage, produce a competitive analysis framework rather than a differentiation statement.

## Downstream handoff
Output can feed:
- gtm positioning and messaging skills
- define-product-vision (revisit if differentiation reveals strategic gaps)

## Instructions
1. Identify the primary competitive alternatives (direct, indirect, status quo).
2. Map key dimensions of comparison relevant to the target segment.
3. Identify where the product wins, loses, and is undifferentiated.
4. Identify the most defensible differentiation angle.
5. Draft a differentiation statement.
6. Identify risks and challenges to sustaining differentiation.

## Output
Provide:
- Competitive landscape summary (direct and indirect alternatives)
- Differentiation map: where you win / lose / are parity
- Primary differentiation angle
- Differentiation statement
- Defensibility assessment (how hard is this to copy?)
- Risks and gaps
- Confidence level: provisional / context-informed / ready-to-use

## Risks / caveats
- Do not claim differentiation in areas where the product is actually at parity
- "Better UX" and "more features" are weak differentiation — push for specificity
- Differentiation must be anchored to what the target segment values
