# Example: Rich Context

## Input

**Product:** B2B HR onboarding platform
**Target segment:** HR teams at companies with 100–1,000 employees undergoing rapid hiring (50+ new hires/year)
**Core value delivered:** Reduce time-to-productivity for new hires by automating onboarding workflows and giving HR visibility into completion
**Business model:** SaaS, per-seat, sold to HR buyers
**Existing draft:** "We help companies onboard people faster."

## Output

### Mission statement
We help HR teams at fast-growing companies run structured, consistent onboarding at scale — so new hires reach full productivity faster and HR spends less time chasing paperwork.

### Who it serves
HR teams (primary buyer and user) at companies with 100–1,000 employees hiring 50+ people per year. Not a fit for teams below 50 hires/year where manual onboarding is manageable.

### Core job or problem addressed
HR teams at this scale can't manually manage onboarding for 50+ hires annually without inconsistency and admin overhead. New hires get a fragmented experience; HR loses hours to status tracking.

### Value delivered
Structured, automated onboarding at scale — consistent experience for new hires, visibility and control for HR, less manual coordination for everyone.

### Specificity test: what would this mission say no to?
- Building peer-connection features (that's a culture tool, not onboarding ops)
- Supporting freelancer or contractor onboarding (different compliance and workflow needs)
- Serving companies doing fewer than 20 hires/year (manual tools suffice; low willingness to pay)
- Building performance management features (out of scope — onboarding ends at productivity, not ongoing performance)

### Confidence level
Context-informed

### Open questions
- Is "full productivity" measurable within the product, or is it defined by the customer?
- Does the mission hold if we expand to mid-market (1,000–5,000 employees)?
