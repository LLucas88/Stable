## Input
We want to add a free tier to our SaaS product. Right now we only have one paid plan. We're thinking free, basic, and pro.

## Expected output
Value metric:
Not defined yet. This is the most important input for tier design — what do customers get more of as they pay more? Common options for SaaS: seats, projects, storage, feature access, usage volume. Needs to be identified before the tier structure is useful.

Tier structure (provisional):

Free:
- Target: not yet defined — who is the free tier for? New users exploring, or a permanent free segment?
- Included: not yet specified
- Upgrade trigger: not designed yet

Basic:
- Target: unclear — "Basic" as a name implies a lower tier, but what segment does it serve?
- Included: not specified
- Risk: three tiers (Free, Basic, Pro) can create confusion if Basic and Free feel too similar

Pro:
- Target: power users or larger teams, presumably
- Included: presumably full feature access

Conversion path:
Not designed. The tier structure needs explicit upgrade triggers — what moment or friction causes a free user to need Basic, or a Basic user to need Pro?

Cannibalization risks:
If the Basic tier is priced too close to Free or includes too much, there's no incentive to upgrade. If Pro is too expensive relative to Basic, the drop-off at that step will be high.

Validation steps:
- Define who the free tier is for before designing what it includes
- Map 2-3 customer segments and their willingness to pay
- Identify the value metric before finalizing what differentiates tiers

Missing information:
- Value metric
- Customer segments and their needs
- Current pricing and plan contents
- Business model (self-serve? enterprise motion?)
- Reason for adding a free tier (acquisition? competitive?)

Confidence level:
Provisional
