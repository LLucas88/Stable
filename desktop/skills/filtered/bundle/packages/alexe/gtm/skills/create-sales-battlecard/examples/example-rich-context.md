## Input
We need a battlecard against Asana. We sell a B2B project management SaaS targeting teams of 10-50 people. Our main strengths: we're significantly cheaper ($8/user vs. Asana's $13.49 Business), our time tracking is built-in (Asana requires an integration), and our onboarding takes under a day (customers regularly tell us Asana took weeks to roll out).

Common competitive objections we hear:
- "Asana has more integrations"
- "Our team is already used to Asana / we used it at our last job"
- "Asana has better reporting"

Where Asana is stronger:
- More third-party integrations (1000+ vs. our ~150)
- Enterprise features (SAML SSO, advanced permissions, audit logs) — we don't have these yet
- Brand recognition — easier to get internal buy-in

Win data: in 14 deals where we beat Asana in the last quarter, the most common reasons were price (11/14) and time tracking (8/14). We lost 6 deals to Asana — 4 cited enterprise security features, 2 cited integrations.

Customer quote: "We tried Asana for two weeks and spent most of that time in the setup. With [us], we were running actual projects on day two." — Head of Operations, 35-person e-commerce company.

## Expected output
Competitor overview:
Asana is a well-established project management platform targeting teams from small to enterprise. Strong brand recognition, large integration library, and mature enterprise features. Positioned as a comprehensive work management platform, which also makes it more complex and expensive for smaller teams.

Common objections and responses:

"Asana has more integrations"
The 1000+ integrations number includes many duplicates and low-usage tools. Ask which specific integrations matter to them. If they need Salesforce, HubSpot, or Slack — we cover those. If they need a niche ERP integration, that's a legitimate gap. Don't defend breadth; focus on what they actually need.

"Our team already knows Asana"
Familiarity is real, but it's a switching cost, not a product advantage. Ask: how long did it take to actually get your team adopting it consistently? Our average onboarding is under one day. If Asana took weeks to roll out at a previous company, that familiarity cost them something. Most habits from Asana transfer — tasks, projects, comments are universal concepts.

"Asana has better reporting"
Ask what they're trying to report on. Asana's reporting requires the Business plan ($13.49/user). Our reporting covers task completion rates, overdue trends, and workload distribution — built-in at $8/user. If they need advanced BI integrations or cross-portfolio views at enterprise scale, that's an honest gap.

Win reasons against Asana:
1. Price: $8 vs. $13.49/user/month at the comparable tier — 40% cheaper. For a 30-person team, that's ~$1,960/year difference.
2. Built-in time tracking: no integration setup, no third-party cost. Teams that bill by the hour or track effort use this from day one.
3. Onboarding speed: customers consistently report being operational within a day. Asana's complexity often requires weeks of configuration and training for full adoption.

Where Asana is stronger and how to handle it:
Enterprise security (SAML SSO, audit logs, advanced permissions): honest answer — we don't have these yet. If security and compliance requirements are enterprise-grade, Asana is the better fit today. Don't oversell a roadmap. Qualify early: ask if they have a security review process or SOC2 requirements.

Integration breadth: genuine gap for niche tools. Qualify which integrations they need before this becomes a blocker. If the integrations they need are in our library, it's not a real objection.

Qualifying questions for competitive deals:
- "Which integrations are non-negotiable for your team?"
- "Do you have enterprise security requirements — SSO, compliance audits?"
- "What did adoption actually look like the last time you rolled out Asana?"
- "How does your team track time spent on projects today?"

Proof points:
"We were running actual projects on day two." — Head of Operations, 35-person e-commerce company
14/14 deals won against Asana in Q4 had price or time tracking as a factor
40% price difference at the comparable tier (Business plan)

Phrases to avoid:
- "Asana is bloated" — sounds defensive
- "Asana is for enterprise only" — not accurate, and buyers will fact-check
- "We do everything Asana does" — we don't, and saying so damages trust
