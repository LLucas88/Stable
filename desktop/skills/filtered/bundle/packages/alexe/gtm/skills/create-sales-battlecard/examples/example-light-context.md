## Input
We keep losing deals to a competitor called Notion. We need a battlecard. We're a B2B SaaS wiki and documentation tool.

## Expected output
Competitor overview (provisional):
Notion is a flexible all-in-one workspace tool used for documentation, wikis, project management, and databases. Popular with startups and individual users; strong brand among tech-forward teams. Primary positioning: everything in one place, fully customizable.

Common objections and responses (provisional — needs win/loss data to validate):

"Notion does everything we need"
Notion is extremely flexible, which is also its problem — it requires significant setup before it's useful. A documentation-first tool like ours is structured from day one. Ask: how long did it take your team to agree on how to use Notion? If the answer is "we never fully agreed," that's the opening.

"Notion is cheaper / we already pay for it"
Notion has a free tier that many teams use, but the Business plan ($16/user/month) adds up. If your pricing is competitive, quantify it. If they're "already paying for it," the question is whether they're actually getting value from the documentation use case or just using it for notes.

"Our team already uses Notion"
Switching cost is real. The question is whether their current Notion setup actually works well for documentation at team scale — or whether it's a mess of half-maintained pages. Position as specialization, not replacement.

Win reasons (to be validated with actual win data):
1. Structure out of the box — no setup time to get documentation working
2. Search that works across all pages without nested structure confusion
3. Permissions designed for team documentation (editor/viewer/team roles)

[Note: these are hypothetical — actual win reasons should come from won deal interviews]

Where Notion is stronger and how to handle it:
- Databases and project management: Notion is genuinely more capable. If the team needs databases and project tracking, Notion has a real advantage. Qualify whether they need documentation specifically or a general-purpose tool.
- Free tier: Notion is free for small teams, which is a real acquisition advantage.

Qualifying questions:
- "How is your team currently using Notion — documentation, project tracking, or both?"
- "When someone on your team needs to find a specific process or policy document, how do they find it?"
- "How much time do you spend maintaining your Notion setup vs. actually creating content?"
