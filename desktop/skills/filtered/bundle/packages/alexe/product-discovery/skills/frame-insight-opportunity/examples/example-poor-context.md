## Input

"We need some opportunities to present to leadership."

## Expected output

Opportunity statements must be grounded in research insights. Producing them without research data creates opportunities that sound structured but embed unverified assumptions — which risks directing leadership investment toward problems that may not exist or solutions that won't work.

A stakeholder presentation built on fabricated opportunities does more harm than no presentation: it creates commitment to an unvalidated direction.

To produce useful opportunity statements, provide:
- Research insights or synthesis (even rough themes count)
- The affected user segment
- The business goal this should connect to

Without these, the only honest output is a template:

Opportunity statement format: "How might we [help {segment} accomplish {job or need}] so that [{outcome for user or business}]?"

Fields to complete:
- Opportunity statement (HMW format): [requires insight]
- Source insight: [requires research]
- Affected segment: [requires segment definition]
- Potential impact: [requires business context]
- Evidence quality: Assumed (until research is provided)
- Suggested next step: [cannot be determined without insight and segment]

Recommended next step: If research exists in any form (interview notes, support tickets, NPS comments), start with synthesize-qualitative-research or generate-insights to produce insights first. Then return to this skill to frame those insights as opportunities.

If no research exists, start with identify-problem-opportunity to define the problem, then scope and run research before framing opportunities.
