## Input

"We need to do some research."

## Expected output

Cannot scope a research initiative without knowing what it is trying to find out or what decision it must inform.

Scoping research without a goal produces a plan that looks structured but leads to findings that cannot be actioned — a common and costly failure mode.

Questions to establish before any scoping is possible:
1. What product area or problem is this research about?
2. What decision are you trying to make after the research is complete?
3. Who are the users you need to understand?
4. How much time and resource is available?

Without answers to these four questions, any scope produced would be arbitrary. The research would collect interesting data that doesn't connect to a product decision.

Recommended next step: Answer the four questions above. If the team isn't sure what decision the research should inform, start with identify-problem-opportunity to establish a problem statement first — then scope research to validate or deepen it.
