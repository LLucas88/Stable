## Input

Product: Fitly — a B2C mobile app for booking fitness classes at local studios (yoga, boxing, pilates, cycling).

Signal: "Our booking rates seem to drop after the first month. We've had a few cancellations but nothing formal."

## Expected output

Problem statement (provisional): Users may be disengaging from Fitly after their first month of use, but the underlying cause has not been identified.

Affected segment: Unknown — likely new users who booked 1–3 classes in their first month, but the specific cohort, behaviour pattern, and segment characteristics are not confirmed.

Raw signal: Team perception of declining booking rates; unspecified cancellations. No data cited.

Business impact: Cannot be assessed without knowing the booking rate baseline, business target, and revenue model (commission per booking, subscription, or class pack).

Evidence quality: Anecdotal — no data provided. Problem framing is a starting hypothesis only.

Open questions to resolve before the problem can be framed:
- What does the booking data actually show — at what point do users stop booking, and is it a gradual decline or a sharp drop?
- Are users cancelling booked classes or simply not booking new ones? These are different problems.
- What product stage are users at when they disengage — did they complete a class, rate the experience, or only browse?
- Is there a business goal this booking gap is blocking?
- Is the pattern concentrated in specific class types, studios, or cities?

Confidence level: Provisional — problem framing requires data before it can move to research or hypothesis.
