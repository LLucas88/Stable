## Input

Product: Calmly — a B2C mobile app for guided meditation and mental wellness, targeting working professionals aged 25–40.

Available research: 9 user interviews completed with active and lapsed users. Key themes from notes:
- Users frequently mentioned the commute home or the 30 minutes after putting kids to bed as the moment they feel the pull to decompress ("I need something to mark the end of the work day")
- Several described wanting to feel "reset" rather than "relaxed" — relaxation felt passive and unearned; resetting felt like a deliberate transition
- Competing alternatives mentioned: YouTube ambient videos ("too much scrolling to find one"), physical exercise ("effective but requires too much energy after a long day"), alcohol ("works immediately but I feel worse the next morning"), and doing nothing ("just doom-scrolling until I fall asleep")
- Lapsed users described sessions as feeling like homework; active users described them as a ritual that separates work-brain from home-brain

## Expected output

Job statement: "When my work day ends and I'm still carrying the mental load, I want a quick, deliberate transition ritual, so I can show up present for the rest of my evening instead of staying in work mode."

Functional dimension: Complete a short guided session (5–15 min) that requires minimal decision-making, provides structure without effort, and produces a noticeable shift in mental state. The task is a transition ritual, not a wellness practice.

Emotional dimension: Feel like a different version of yourself after the session. Move from "wired and scattered" to "calm and present." Avoid the guilt of passive coping (scrolling, drinking) by doing something that feels intentional and healthy.

Social dimension: Be the kind of person who manages stress deliberately rather than numbing it. The perceived identity is quiet self-care without the performative wellness aesthetic. Users did not want to be seen as "meditation people" — they wanted to be seen as people who handle pressure well.

Struggling moment: The transition between work and personal time — specifically the 15–60 minutes after work ends, when the mental residue of the day is still active. Secondary struggling moment: lying in bed unable to fall asleep because the mind is still processing work.

Competing solutions for the same job:
- YouTube ambient videos: Accessible but require active search and selection at a moment when decision fatigue is high; no structure, no defined endpoint
- Physical exercise: Effective reset but demands energy users don't have at that moment; feels like another obligation
- Alcohol: Immediate effect, zero effort, but creates next-day regret and doesn't produce the "clean reset" users describe wanting
- Doing nothing (scrolling): Default when all other options feel like too much effort; produces guilt rather than relief

Design implications: Calmly should position itself as a transition tool, not a meditation app. The core experience should minimise choice (no browsing, no decision-making) and deliver a short, structured session that users can start within seconds of opening the app. The "end of work day" moment is the primary activation trigger. Onboarding and notifications should be anchored to this moment rather than to a generic daily reminder.

Evidence quality: Interview-based — consistent pattern across 9 interviews; functional and emotional jobs well-supported; social job inferred from language about identity and self-perception but not explicitly stated by users.

Open questions:
- Does this primary job apply equally to users without a clear work/home boundary (remote workers, freelancers)?
- Is the bedtime struggling moment a distinct job or the same job occurring at a different time?
- Is there a secondary job around anxiety management during acute stress moments (before a presentation, after a conflict) that serves a different use pattern?
