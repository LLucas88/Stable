## Input

Product: Vendora — a B2B SaaS platform for procurement teams.

Context: "We think procurement managers use Vendora because it saves them time on vendor comparisons. We ran a couple of calls with customers last quarter but didn't document them properly."

## Expected output

Job statement (provisional): "When I need to evaluate vendors for a purchase, I want to quickly compare options with reliable data, so I can make a defensible recommendation without spending days on manual research."

Note: This is a hypothesis job statement based on a team assumption. It should not be treated as a validated finding until customer calls are formally analysed.

Functional dimension (assumed): Search for vendors, compare pricing and capabilities, generate a comparison document or shortlist. Exact workflow and frequency of use unknown.

Emotional dimension (assumed): Reduce the anxiety of making a wrong vendor recommendation that reflects poorly on the buyer. Feel confident presenting a shortlist to leadership. What specifically triggers that anxiety and what resolves it is not confirmed.

Social dimension: Not identified. Procurement roles often have internal reputation stakes (choosing a vendor that fails reflects on the buyer), but this would need to be confirmed through interviews.

Struggling moment (provisional): Receiving a purchase request with a tight deadline and no prior vendor research. But the specific trigger moment and the urgency pattern have not been established from data.

Competing solutions: Unknown until customer call data is reviewed. Likely candidates: manual web research, asking peers, relying on existing vendor relationships, or delegating to consultants.

Evidence quality: Assumed — customer call data exists but was not documented. The job statement reflects team perception, not customer language.

Open questions:
- What specific moment triggers the job — a new budget cycle, a specific purchase request, a contract renewal?
- What does "saving time" mean in practice — reducing research hours, reducing approval cycles, or reducing decision risk?
- Are there distinct jobs for different buyer types (first-time buyers vs. experienced procurement managers vs. finance approvers)?

Recommended next step: Re-interview 4–6 customers using this framework. Map each observation to functional, emotional, and social dimensions. Focus on the moment that triggers vendor evaluation and what the buyer is really trying to accomplish beyond the stated task.
