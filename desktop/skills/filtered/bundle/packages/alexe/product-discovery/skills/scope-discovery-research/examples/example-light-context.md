## Input

Product: Flowdesk — a B2B SaaS project management tool for small agencies (5–30 people).

Context: "We want to do some research on why teams stop using Flowdesk after the trial period. We have a few weeks to do it. We're not sure exactly what we want to learn yet."

## Expected output

Research goal (provisional): Understand why trial teams do not convert to paid Flowdesk subscriptions — but the specific decision this research should inform has not yet been defined, which limits how the scope can be finalised.

Questions to confirm before research scope can be set:
1. What decision will this research inform — pricing changes, onboarding redesign, feature prioritisation, or positioning?
2. At what point in the trial do teams disengage — is it early (first few days), mid-trial, or at the payment wall?
3. Who are the most important users to understand — the team admin who chose Flowdesk, the team members who used it daily, or both?
4. Are there existing hypotheses the team wants to explore or stress-test?

Preliminary direction (subject to clarification above):
- Likely method: Semi-structured interviews with trial team admins (the decision-maker who chose to try Flowdesk)
- Likely participants: 6–8 teams that completed at least one week of the trial but did not convert
- Likely questions: What prompted them to try Flowdesk, what they experienced during the trial, what caused them to not convert
- Timeline: Feasible in 3–4 weeks, but output format and decision criteria cannot be defined without a clearer goal

Risk: Without a decision goal, findings will be interesting but may not produce a clear product recommendation. Trial conversion research is particularly prone to this: it often surfaces a mix of pricing, feature, and onboarding issues without a framework for prioritising between them.

Recommended next step: Answer the four clarifying questions above, then return to finalise the research scope with defined goal, questions, and output format.

Confidence level: Partial scope — direction set, but not ready to execute.
