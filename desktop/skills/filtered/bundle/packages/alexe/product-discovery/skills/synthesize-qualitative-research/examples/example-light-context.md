## Input

Product: Cardy — a B2C mobile app for creating and sharing digital business cards.

Context: "We did about 5 interviews with users who stopped using the app. People said they created a card but didn't really use it afterward. A couple mentioned wanting to share it but finding it awkward. We didn't record the sessions so we're working from memory."

## Expected output

Research questions (inferred): Why do users stop using Cardy after creating their first digital card? What prevents ongoing engagement?

Key themes (provisional — memory-based, not verified):
- Card creation may be a one-time action with no follow-up use case. Users completed the core task (create a card) but had no reason to return afterward.
- Sharing a digital card in person may feel socially awkward. At least some users described discomfort around the sharing moment, but the exact context and frequency are not documented.

Top insights (provisional — low reliability, treat as hypotheses only):
1. Users create a digital card but do not return because the app provides no ongoing value beyond the initial creation. The core action is a single event, not a recurring need.
2. The sharing experience may be a friction point. If users feel awkward sharing their card in face-to-face situations, the product's core value loop (create → share → connect) breaks at step two.

Outliers: Unknown — without notes, outlying responses cannot be identified or separated from the main pattern.

Research gaps: Almost everything that would be needed to act on this:
- No transcripts or structured notes; recall bias is likely and unquantifiable
- Unable to distinguish response patterns between participants
- The "awkward" sharing feedback is not detailed enough to design a solution: what was awkward — the format, the social norm, the technical mechanism?
- No data on users who do share regularly — what makes their experience different?
- No contrast with retained users

Confidence level: Thin-data — this synthesis is directional only. No product decisions should be made based on this alone.

Recommended next step: Conduct 5–8 additional structured interviews with notes or recording. Include 3–4 users who actively share their card for contrast. Probe specifically on the sharing moment: when does it happen, what triggers it, what makes it feel natural or awkward, and what happens after the card is shared.
