## Input

Product: Packly — a B2C meal kit subscription service delivering pre-portioned ingredients and recipes.

Context: "We think we have two types of customers: busy parents who want dinner solved and food enthusiasts who like trying new recipes. We haven't validated this with research yet."

## Expected output

Persona 1 (provisional — assumption-based): The Convenience Buyer

Name and archetype: To be confirmed in research

Primary goals and JTBD (assumed):
- Goal: Eliminate the daily decision of what to cook for dinner without resorting to takeout
- JTBD (hypothesised): "When I get home from work and need to feed my family, I want dinner to be decided and prepped for me, so I can spend less mental energy on meal planning."

Key pain points (assumed): Suspected friction around meal variety fatigue and delivery timing. Not confirmed.

Behavioural patterns: Assumed to value speed of preparation over recipe complexity; likely to skip weeks when schedule is unusually busy. Not yet confirmed from data.

Evidence quality: Assumed — based on team intuition. Not validated.

Research gaps: What specifically makes meal planning stressful for this group: the decision, the shopping, or the cooking? Does this persona care about recipe variety or would they prefer a smaller, repeating menu? Is price or convenience the primary retention driver?

---

Persona 2 (provisional — assumption-based): The Cooking Explorer

Name and archetype: To be confirmed in research

Primary goals and JTBD (assumed):
- Goal: Try new cuisines and techniques without the overhead of sourcing specialty ingredients
- JTBD (hypothesised): "When I want to cook something new and interesting, I want the ingredients and instructions ready so I can focus on the experience of cooking rather than the logistics of shopping."

Key pain points (assumed): Suspected frustration when recipes feel too simple or when ingredient quality doesn't meet expectations. Not confirmed.

Behavioural patterns: Assumed to engage more with recipe content, rate dishes, and prefer varied menus. Not yet confirmed.

Evidence quality: Assumed — based on team intuition. Not validated.

Research gaps: Is this a truly distinct segment or a mood that all subscribers experience occasionally? Does this persona churn when recipes become repetitive, or do other factors drive cancellation? Would a premium tier with more adventurous recipes retain this segment better?

---

Note: These are hypothesis personas only. Do not use for product, design, or messaging decisions until validated with research. Recommended next step: run 4–6 interviews with subscribers matching each suspected type to validate motivations, pain points, and context of use before treating these as decision-ready segments.
