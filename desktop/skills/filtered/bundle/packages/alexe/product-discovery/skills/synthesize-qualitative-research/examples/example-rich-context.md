## Input

Product: Chefable — a B2C mobile app combining a recipe library with a cooking community where users share recipes, post meal photos, and follow other home cooks.

Research conducted: 10 user interviews (5 churned users — no session in last 30 days; 5 retained users — 3+ sessions per week). All participants had posted at least one recipe or meal photo during their time on the app.

Research questions: (1) Why do active contributors stop engaging? (2) What moments feel most rewarding? (3) What role does community interaction play in retention?

Interview notes summary:
- "I posted a few things and nobody reacted. It felt like shouting into a void." — 4 of 5 churned users
- "When someone comments on my recipe and says they tried it, that's the best feeling." — 4 of 5 retained users
- "I started using it for recipes but I stay because of the people I follow. It's like a quiet little food world." — 3 of 5 retained users
- "The recipe search is fine but I can get that from any website. I didn't need an app for it." — 3 of 10 users
- "I saved a bunch of recipes but never cooked any of them. It became another bookmarking app." — 3 of 5 churned users
- All 5 retained users had received at least 3 comments or reactions on their posts in the first two weeks. Only 1 of 5 churned users had.
- 4 of 5 retained users followed at least 5 other users. No churned user followed more than 2.

Prior assumption the team held: Churn was assumed to be caused by recipe content quality — the team believed users left because they couldn't find recipes that matched their dietary preferences.

## Expected output

Research questions: (1) Why do active contributors stop engaging? (2) What moments feel most rewarding? (3) What role does community interaction play in retention?

Key themes:

Theme 1 — Community feedback is the retention mechanism, not recipes: Retained users described social interaction (comments, reactions, followers) as the reason they return. Churned users described the absence of interaction as the reason they stopped. The recipe library alone does not create a compelling return reason.

Theme 2 — Early social validation determines contributor retention: All 5 retained users received 3+ community reactions in their first two weeks. Only 1 of 5 churned users did. The presence or absence of early feedback appears to set the trajectory for long-term engagement.

Theme 3 — Recipe saving without cooking creates a bookmarking trap: 3 of 5 churned users described saving recipes but never cooking them. The save action felt productive but didn't lead to actual engagement with the product's core value (cooking and sharing). Saved recipes became dead inventory.

Theme 4 — Following other users creates a content feed worth returning to: 4 of 5 retained users followed 5+ other users. This transformed the app from a search tool into a feed of content from people they cared about. No churned user built this network.

Theme 5 — Recipe search alone is not a differentiator: 3 of 10 users noted that recipe search is available on many free websites. The app's value must come from something beyond content access — the community layer is where differentiation exists.

Top insights:
1. Community feedback in the first two weeks is the strongest predictor of contributor retention — users who receive reactions stay; users who post into silence leave.
2. The recipe library is a hygiene feature, not a differentiator. Users can get recipes anywhere. The reason to use Chefable is the community, but the product's onboarding and discovery emphasise recipes over people.
3. Recipe saving creates an illusion of engagement without producing actual value — users who only save without cooking or sharing are on a path to churn.
4. Following other users transforms the app experience from search-based to feed-based. This transition is the activation moment, but the product does not guide users toward it.
5. The emotional reward of cooking community is asymmetric: contributing and receiving feedback is deeply satisfying; browsing passively is not compelling enough to retain.

JTBD summary: Primary job appears to be "share what I cook and feel appreciated by people who care about food." The app succeeds when it creates a feedback loop between cooking, posting, and receiving community response. It fails when users treat it as a recipe database.

Outliers and edge cases: One retained user had never posted a recipe but remained highly active as a commenter and follower. They described Chefable as "the only social media I don't feel bad about using." This may represent a distinct "passive community participant" segment that engages through consumption and commenting rather than content creation.

Research gaps:
- No data on how discovery of other users works — are retained users finding people through search, recommendations, or off-platform connections?
- Dietary preference matching not explored — the prior assumption about content quality may still be relevant for a subset of users
- No data on whether push notifications for reactions drive return visits or are ignored
- Sample did not include users who never posted — the experience of pure consumers is not represented

Confidence level: Moderate — 10 interviews; consistent pattern across both groups; early feedback finding corroborated by product engagement data. Limitation: churned users contacted retrospectively; recall bias possible. Community size and density effects not controlled for.
