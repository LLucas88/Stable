## Input
We need a product strategy doc. We're a SaaS company, we want to focus on retention and growing our enterprise segment. We have about 500 customers.

## Expected output
Context (as understood):
SaaS product, 500 customers, two stated priorities: improve retention and grow the enterprise segment. Current state, metrics, and specific context not yet provided.

A strategy doc with "focus on retention and enterprise" as its basis is not a strategy — it's a goal list. A meaningful strategy doc requires choices: why these over other priorities, what you're giving up, and what "success" looks like specifically.

---

Provisional structure (to be filled in with real context):

**Context**
[What is the product? What is the current business situation — revenue, growth rate, key customer type? What problem or opportunity is this strategy responding to?]

**Strategic approach and key choices**
- Why retention over acquisition focus: [your reasoning — e.g., current retention metrics, CAC payback period, cohort data]
- Why enterprise over SMB growth: [your reasoning — is SMB growth slowing? Is enterprise showing stronger LTV? Is there a specific opportunity in the market?]

**Explicit trade-offs and what's out of scope**
- What are you explicitly not doing to focus on these two priorities?
- If you're growing enterprise, are you deprioritizing SMB-specific features? Are you accepting slower expansion of the SMB base?
- If you're investing in retention, are you deferring acquisition or marketing investment?

**Success definition (12-month horizon)**
- Retention: what metric, from what to what, by when?
- Enterprise: what counts as "growing" — number of accounts, ARR, ACV?

**Key risks**
- If enterprise sales cycles are long and retention improvements are slow to show, what's the 6-month early signal you'll watch?

---

Missing context before this document can be meaningful:
- Current retention rate and what you believe drives churn
- Current enterprise account count and ARR, and what "growth" looks like in 12 months
- What makes your product valuable to enterprise customers vs. SMB (if they use it differently, strategy differs)
- Engineering and product team capacity — strategy choices must be feasible

Once this context is available, the doc can be written in final form. Without it, the strategy will be generic enough to apply to any SaaS company — which means it won't actually help your team make decisions.
