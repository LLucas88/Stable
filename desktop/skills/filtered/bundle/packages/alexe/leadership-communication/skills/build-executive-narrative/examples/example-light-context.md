## Input
I need to present our product roadmap to leadership next month. We've been working on some big features and want to show them where we're headed.

## Expected output
Core message (not yet defined — needs to be decided before the narrative can be structured):

What should leadership take away from this presentation? Options:
- "Here is our plan for the next 6 months and why we made these bets"
- "We need your approval / alignment on this direction before we execute"
- "We've made good progress on our strategy and here's where we're going next"

The narrative arc depends on which of these is the real purpose.

---

Provisional narrative arc (assuming the goal is alignment, not just information):

**Context**
What is the current state? What metrics, customer situation, or strategic reality is this roadmap responding to?

**Insight**
What have we learned that shapes these roadmap choices? (Customer research, metric trends, competitive moves)

**Direction**
What are the 2–3 biggest bets on the roadmap and why? (Not a feature list — why these over everything else)

**Ask (if any)**
Do you need leadership to approve resources, unblock dependencies, or align on trade-offs? Name it explicitly.

---

What needs to be defined before structuring the narrative:
- Audience: are these operational leads who know the product, or board members who don't? Changes the level of detail and framing significantly.
- The core message: what is the one thing you want leadership to leave the meeting knowing or believing?
- Key data: what results or research support the roadmap choices? Executive presentations without data supporting the direction are harder to defend.
- The ask (if any): what does leadership need to decide, approve, or be aware of?

---

Common mistakes to avoid:
- Presenting a feature list instead of strategic choices (a roadmap is strategy; don't make it a backlog walkthrough)
- Leading with data before the story (show what matters and why, then support with data)
- Avoiding difficult trade-offs (if the roadmap deprioritized something important, name it and explain why — leadership will ask)
