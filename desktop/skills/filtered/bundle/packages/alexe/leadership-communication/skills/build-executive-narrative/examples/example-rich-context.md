## Input
I need to present to the CEO and board next week on our product progress for Q2. Here's the context:

- Q2 goals: increase activation rate from 31% to 45%, reduce D30 churn from 18% to 13%
- Q2 results: activation is at 38% (short of goal), D30 churn improved to 14% (close to goal)
- What drove the results: we shipped 2 of 3 planned onboarding improvements. The third (goal-setting step) was delayed 4 weeks due to an unplanned infrastructure migration.
- Q3 outlook: the delayed goal-setting step is scheduled to ship week 2 of Q3. Our model suggests it could add 5–7 points to activation if our hypothesis holds.
- A difficult truth: activation improvement is slowing. The easy improvements are done. The next gains require deeper product work and likely a personalization investment.
- What I need from the board: approval to add 1 engineer to the personalization workstream in Q4.

Audience: CEO + 3 board members. Two board members are not product people; they think in terms of revenue, retention, and market position.

## Expected output
Core message (one sentence):
Q2 retention improved meaningfully; activation made progress but fell short of target, and the path to the next level requires a focused personalization investment.

---

Narrative arc outline:

**Context (slide 1–2)**
Where we were at the start of Q2: activation at 31%, D30 churn at 18%. These were the two metrics most correlated with 12-month LTV in our cohort analysis.

**Insight (slide 3–4)**
What we learned in Q2:
- Churn responded well to the first wave of onboarding changes (D30 churn: 18% → 14%)
- Activation improved from 31% to 38%, but the goal of 45% wasn't reached because the third and most impactful onboarding change was delayed
- The delay was caused by an unplanned infrastructure migration (2 weeks). Not a product quality problem.
- Key learning: the first activation gains came from reducing friction. The next gains require relevance: helping users understand why the product matters for their specific situation. That requires personalization.

**Direction (slide 5)**
Q3 plan: ship the delayed goal-setting step in week 2, run an A/B test, evaluate the 5–7 point activation hypothesis by end of month. If hypothesis is confirmed, begin Q4 personalization investment.

What we're NOT doing: chasing the 45% activation number through quick hacks that erode product quality.

**Ask (slide 6)**
One additional engineer added to the team in Q4, dedicated to personalization infrastructure. Estimated cost: [$X fully-loaded]. Expected return: if personalization achieves a 5+ point activation improvement, the LTV impact over 12 months is [$Y; calculate this before the presentation].

---

Supporting data points (max 5, most important):
1. D30 churn: 18% → 14% (close to 13% goal)
2. 7-day activation: 31% → 38% (7 point improvement; 45% target missed)
3. Goal-setting step delayed 4 weeks; ships week 2 Q3
4. Activation improvement from reducing friction: ~7 points. Activation improvement from relevance/personalization: estimated 8–12 points (hypothesis, not confirmed)
5. Current activation rate puts us 12 points behind the top 3 competitors in our category (if this data exists; use only if reliable)

---

Challenge framing:
"We didn't hit 45% activation. The 7-point improvement was real. The remaining gap requires a different approach: personalization, which we couldn't ship in Q2. We know what the path is. We're asking for the resource to execute it."

Don't say: "We had some challenges but we're optimistic about Q3."
Don't hide: the infrastructure delay. Name it, explain it, and explain why it won't happen again.

---

Desired outcome from the communication:
Approval for Q4 engineer headcount, and board confidence that the team understands the problem and has a credible plan.

---

Slide structure:
1. Q2 goals and results summary (one table: goal / result / delta)
2. What drove churn improvement (2–3 bullets)
3. Why activation fell short of target (honest, specific)
4. Q3 plan and the goal-setting step test
5. The personalization case: why friction removal isn't enough for the next activation gains
6. The ask: Q4 engineer + expected return

---

What to avoid saying:
- "We made great progress": mixed results, not a great quarter; be honest
- "We're confident we'll hit our targets next quarter": confidence without evidence sounds like spin
- "The infrastructure migration was a one-off": say what changed to prevent recurrence; don't just call it a one-off
