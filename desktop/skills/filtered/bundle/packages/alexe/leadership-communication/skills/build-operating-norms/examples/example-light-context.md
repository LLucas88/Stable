## Input
Our team is 5 people and we have some friction around meetings and communication. We want to write some team norms.

## Expected output
Norm domains (draft — starting points based on most common team friction areas):

Before writing specific norms, the key friction points need to be named. "Friction around meetings and communication" could mean many things:
- Too many meetings, or the wrong people in meetings
- Unclear communication channels (is this in email? Slack? Both?)
- Decisions made in meetings that other people didn't know about
- Response time expectations that aren't shared

Without knowing what specifically is going wrong, the norms will be generic.

---

Draft norms to discuss with the team:

**Meetings**
- Meetings have a stated purpose and owner. No agenda = meeting gets cancelled or moved to async.
- Optional attendees are labeled clearly. Team members who don't need to contribute can skip without explanation.
- Decisions from meetings are summarized in writing within 24 hours and shared in a common channel.

**Communication channels**
- Define one channel for: time-sensitive requests, project updates, social/informal. If everything goes to one Slack channel, nothing gets appropriate attention.
- Response expectation: acknowledge messages within [X hours] during working hours. Not a response — just an acknowledgment that you've seen it.

**Decision visibility**
- If a decision affects someone else's work, they get 24 hours to weigh in before it's final.

---

What should happen before finalizing these norms:
- Team conversation: ask each person "what's one thing about how we work that frustrates you?" These norms should respond to real friction, not imagined problems.
- Agreement: norms created by one person for others are rules, not agreements. The team should discuss and adjust before these become official.

---

Missing context that would sharpen the norms:
- What specifically is causing the friction? (Meeting scheduling? Too many meetings? Communication channels overlap? Response time ambiguity?)
- Remote vs. in-person vs. hybrid? (Changes which communication norms matter most)
- What are the team's current tools? (Slack, email, Notion, other — affects channel norms)
