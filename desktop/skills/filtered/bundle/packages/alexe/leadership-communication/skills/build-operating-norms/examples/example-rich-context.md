## Input
Our product team of 7 (4 engineers, 1 designer, 1 PM, 1 data analyst) has been together for 8 months. We're hybrid — 3 people in the same city, 4 remote across 2 time zones (UTC and UTC+3). We had a retro 2 months ago and the top friction points were:

1. Decisions keep getting made and then relitigated in Slack — people who weren't in the original meeting contest decisions they weren't part of
2. Feedback on design work is chaotic — designers get feedback from 6 people at different stages with contradictory opinions
3. Work hours are ambiguous — some people are available from 7am, others from 11am, and meeting scheduling causes tension
4. New team members (2 joined in the last 3 months) didn't know how the team operates

Team values we've stated: speed, craft, psychological safety.

## Expected output
Norm domains covered: communication, decision-making, feedback process, availability and work hours, onboarding

---

Explicit norm statements:

**Communication**
1. Async-first: status updates, non-urgent questions, and context-sharing happen in writing (Slack or Notion), not in meetings. Meetings are for decisions and alignment, not information delivery.
2. Decision visibility: every decision above a day's work in impact gets a written record (Notion decision log, 3 sentences minimum: what was decided, who was involved, why). Written within 24 hours of the decision.
3. Slack response expectation: messages in #team-general are acknowledged within 4 hours during working hours. DMs are not assumed to be urgent — if urgent, use the @urgent tag.

**Decision-making**
4. Who decides: each functional area has a default owner. PM owns product decisions. Lead engineer owns technical decisions. Designer owns UX decisions. Default owner can request input but is not required to achieve consensus.
5. Challenging decisions: if someone disagrees with a decision, they have 48 hours after the decision is written to raise a concern. After 48 hours, the decision is final and Slack challenges are not appropriate.
6. Reversibility: distinguishing reversible and irreversible decisions matters. Reversible decisions can be made quickly by the default owner. Irreversible decisions (major architecture, significant team structure) require broader input and PM or lead approval.

**Feedback process**
7. Design feedback timing: design work has two designated feedback windows — (a) concept review when 40% done, feedback on direction only; (b) final review when 90% done, feedback on execution only. Feedback outside these windows is noted but not acted on for current work.
8. Single feedback owner: for each design initiative, there is one feedback owner (usually PM). Other team members share feedback with the feedback owner, not directly with the designer, unless the designer explicitly invites it.

**Availability and work hours**
9. Core hours: all team members are available 11am–4pm UTC. Meetings are scheduled only in this window unless explicitly agreed otherwise.
10. Individual hours visibility: each person posts their working hours in Slack status or Notion profile. No expectation of immediate response outside personal working hours.
11. Same-day turnaround: anything marked "needs response today" by 12pm UTC will be responded to the same day. Requests arriving after 3pm UTC may get next-business-day response.

**Onboarding**
12. New team member integration: each new joiner receives this norms document on day 1. A buddy (team member of their choice) is available for the first 30 days for norm and context questions.

---

How norms were co-created (or should be):
These norms were drafted based on the retro findings and should be reviewed with the full team before finalizing. Schedule a 45-minute session: share the draft 48 hours in advance, run a round of async comments, then a 20-minute live review to resolve disagreements. No norm is finalized until the team agrees.

Norm review cadence:
- Quarterly mention in retro: are any norms being violated repeatedly, or have they become irrelevant?
- After significant team changes (new member, team structure change): re-review relevant sections
- Version this document (v1, date). Changes are logged with the reason.

Accountability approach for norm violations:
- First occurrence: direct, private message to the person. No escalation. Assume good intent.
- Recurring pattern: raised in the next 1:1 between PM and the person; addressed as a process issue, not a character issue.
- If a norm is being violated by many people repeatedly, it's a sign the norm was wrong, not that the team is wrong — revisit it.
