## Input
We're launching a new pricing tier for enterprise customers. I need to figure out who to align with. Main stakeholders I know: Sales, Finance, Engineering, Legal.

## Expected output
Stakeholder inventory with role, interest, influence, stance (provisional — needs details to complete):

| Stakeholder | Likely interest | Likely influence | Stance (unknown) |
|------------|----------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Sales | High — new tier directly affects their pipeline and commission structure | High — can make or kill the launch in practice if they don't push it | Unknown |
| Finance | Medium — pricing changes affect revenue model and financial projections | High — pricing decisions require financial sign-off | Unknown |
| Engineering | Low-medium — depends on whether the tier requires new feature flags or billing logic | Medium — timeline dependency if changes are needed | Unknown |
| Legal | Low-medium — contract terms for enterprise may need updating | Medium-high — can block or delay launch if contract review takes time | Unknown |

---

What's missing before this map is useful:
- What are the specific stances of each stakeholder? (Supportive? Skeptical? Neutral?) Without knowing this, engagement strategy can't be tailored.
- Who else should be included? Common stakeholders missed in enterprise pricing: Customer Success (manages existing accounts, handles upsell conversations), Head of Product or CPO (pricing is a product decision), existing enterprise customers if you're migrating them to a new tier.
- What is the decision being made vs. communicated? Is the pricing tier already decided and this is about rollout, or is it still being defined? This changes who needs to input vs. who just needs to be informed.

---

Engagement strategy draft:

**High priority (manage closely):**
- Sales: they execute the tier; their buy-in on positioning and pricing rationale is critical. Brief them before launch, not at launch. Get their input on how the new tier compares to customer objections they hear.
- Finance: get their sign-off on the revenue model and pricing logic before finalizing — not at the end.

**Medium priority (manage carefully):**
- Legal: provide a clear scope of what contract changes are needed. Legal delays are often caused by unclear asks, not deliberate slowness. Give them specific questions, not general "please review."

**Lower priority (inform at milestones):**
- Engineering: align on what backend changes the new tier requires and when. Don't engage them extensively unless there is a real engineering dependency.

---

Recommended next step:
1:1 conversations with Sales lead and Finance lead in the next week to understand their current stance and any concerns. These conversations will surface blockers earlier and make the launch plan more realistic.
