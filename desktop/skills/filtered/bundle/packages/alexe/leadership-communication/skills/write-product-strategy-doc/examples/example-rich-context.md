## Input
I need to write a product strategy doc for our team. We run a mobile-first marketplace for gaming boosting services. Here's the context:

Current situation:
- 18 months old, $2.1M ARR, growing ~8% MoM but slowing
- Key players: buyers (gamers who want to rank up), sellers (professional players/boosters)
- Two biggest problems: (1) new buyer activation is weak — 38% of buyers who place an order don't come back; (2) seller supply is thinning in high-rank brackets because our take rate (25%) is losing top sellers to direct Discord transactions
- Core differentiator so far: safety guarantees (account safety, boosting method quality, dispute resolution)

Strategic choices we've made:
- Focus on retention of existing buyers over pure acquisition (CAC is high and paid acquisition is not profitable yet)
- Keep the safety and quality guarantee as our core positioning — we're not competing on price
- Build supply-side loyalty: reduce dependency on top 20 sellers by growing the mid-tier seller base
- Not building a mobile app for Android in the next 12 months (iOS only)

Time horizon: 12 months

## Expected output
Context: product area, current state, opportunity

We're a marketplace for gaming boosting services: buyers get rank progression with guaranteed safety, sellers earn consistently without managing client acquisition. We hit $2.1M ARR with strong organic growth but growth is decelerating. The constraint is not awareness — it's retention. A buyer's second order is 4x more likely to lead to a long-term relationship than the first, but 62% of first-order buyers don't return.

On the supply side, our take rate is causing top-seller attrition to direct channels. A marketplace that loses its best-performing sellers loses quality assurance — which is our only real differentiator.

---

Strategic approach and key choices:

**Bet 1: Make the second order inevitable, not accidental.**

First-order buyers who don't return are the single largest revenue leak. We're choosing to invest in post-order retention (personalized rebooking offers, streak mechanics, order result communication) over top-of-funnel acquisition. This is a deliberate trade: slower new user growth in exchange for higher LTV per user.

What this means in practice: Q2–Q3 product investment goes into the post-order experience. Acquisition features (referral program, SEO improvements) are Q4 or later.

**Bet 2: Build supply-side loyalty for mid-tier sellers before we lose them too.**

Our top 20 sellers generate 38% of revenue but are the most likely to defect to direct Discord channels. Instead of chasing them with lower take rates (a race we can't win), we're growing the mid-tier seller base (500–1,000 sellers in the right rank range) who value the platform's demand and safety guarantees over maximum per-order earnings.

Mid-tier seller investment: improved seller onboarding, earnings visibility tools, and a seller reputation system that creates platform-specific value they can't replicate off-platform.

**Bet 3: Double down on safety as the differentiator, not features.**

We're not building the most features. We're building the most trusted marketplace. Investment in detection systems (account safety, booster quality checks) makes our guarantee credible, not just stated.

---

Explicit trade-offs and what's out of scope:

Out of scope for the next 12 months:
- Android app: iOS is 72% of sessions; the investment to build and maintain Android parity would split limited engineering capacity
- Price-based competition: we are not reducing take rate to retain top sellers; we're building platform value instead
- Enterprise/esports B2B: potentially valuable but requires a different sales motion and team structure; not ready
- Marketplace expansion to other games: our quality guarantee depends on deep expertise in the current game roster; expanding prematurely would dilute it

What we're explicitly choosing not to do as much as what we're choosing to do: saying no to acquisition features in H1 is a cost — it means slower user growth. We accept that cost.

---

Success definition (12-month horizon):

- Buyer second-order rate: from 38% to 52%
- Mid-tier seller active base: from current level to +200 sellers in target rank brackets
- Monthly churn rate (seller): reduce top-20 seller attrition from current ~4/month to <1.5/month
- Take rate: hold at 25% (no reduction) without accelerating seller defection

If the second-order rate reaches 52% and seller attrition is controlled, $3.2–3.5M ARR within 12 months is achievable without significant acquisition investment.

---

Key risks and mitigations:

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|-----------|
| Buyer retention efforts don't move the needle fast enough | Run A/B tests on individual retention interventions, not a full redesign; maintain early signal on what's working by week 6 |
| Top-seller attrition accelerates before mid-tier supply is ready | Monitor top-20 seller activity weekly; if 3+ defect in a month, escalate to an emergency supply-side intervention |
| Game meta changes reduce booster demand in core game categories | Monitor game health monthly; maintain diversification across 4–5 active titles |

---

Open decisions requiring alignment:

1. Take rate flexibility: if a top-10 seller explicitly threatens to leave, does leadership want the option to offer an individual rate reduction as an exception? This must be decided before it becomes a live situation.
2. Q4 acquisition investment: if second-order rate improves to 48%+ by Q3, does budget shift to acquisition features in Q4 or continue retention investment?
