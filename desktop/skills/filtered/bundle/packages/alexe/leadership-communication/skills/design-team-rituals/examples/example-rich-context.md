## Input
I lead a product team of 6: 3 engineers, 1 designer, 1 data analyst, and me as PM. Hybrid — 2 people in Cyprus, 4 remote across UTC, UTC+2, UTC+3. We've been together 4 months.

Current rituals:
- Daily standup (30 min, 5 people attend, one engineer always skips — they're in a different timezone)
- Weekly product review (60 min, usually just me presenting updates to eng)
- No retro since Q1

Team goals: Q2 activation improvement (ongoing), high-paced delivery (2-week sprints), high psychological safety.

Key challenges: the designer feels out of the loop between handoffs. The data analyst isn't sure when to share findings proactively vs. waiting to be asked. Engineers want less process, more building time.

## Expected output
Team needs assessment:
- Alignment: team is growing and needs shared context on direction and progress
- Feedback: designer and analyst don't have clear moments to share work in progress; async doesn't fill this gap well
- Decision-making rhythm: decisions are happening ad hoc; no clear weekly cadence
- Psychological safety: team is new — trust is still forming; no structured space for it yet
- Delivery discipline: 2-week sprints need clear start/end rituals

---

Current ritual audit:

| Ritual | Attendance | Value | Problem |
|--------|-----------|-------|---------|
| Daily standup (30 min) | 5/6 | Low to medium | Too long for status; one engineer excluded by timezone; becoming a reporting ritual, not a collaboration ritual |
| Weekly product review (60 min) | Full team | Medium | One-way information flow; passive for engineers and analyst |
| Retro | None | Missing | No structured learning or feedback loop |

---

Recommended ritual set:

**1. Daily async standup (replace current 30-min sync)**
Purpose: lightweight status and blocker surfacing
Format: each person posts 3 bullets in Slack by 10am UTC: what did I do yesterday, what am I doing today, any blockers. PM reviews and responds within 1 hour.
Cadence: daily, Monday–Friday
Duration: 5 minutes per person (writing, not meeting)
Who: all 6 team members including the timezone-remote engineer
Facilitation: self-service; PM monitors
Why: removes the timezone exclusion problem; reduces meeting overhead by ~2.5 hours/week

**2. Weekly sync (replace product review) — 45 minutes, Tuesdays at 11am UTC**
Purpose: real-time alignment, decisions, and early work-in-progress review
Format:
- 10 min: sprint progress and blockers (rotating facilitator, not always PM)
- 15 min: one person shares current work in progress (design, data finding, engineering challenge) — everyone gives input
- 10 min: decisions that need the full team
- 10 min: open (overflow or skip)
Cadence: weekly
Who: full team
Why: addresses the designer and analyst inclusion problem; reduces PM as sole presenter; builds team investment in each other's work

**3. Bi-weekly retro — 60 minutes, last Friday of each sprint**
Purpose: honest reflection and improvement
Format: Start / Stop / Continue. First 10 minutes: silent writing. PM reviews prior retro actions first.
Cadence: every 2 weeks (aligned to sprint end)
Facilitation: rotating; PM facilitates first 3 months, then hands off
Why: no retro for months means friction is accumulating unaddressed. Team is new enough that psychological safety rituals matter now, not later.

**4. Monthly async data share**
Purpose: analyst surfaces findings proactively
Format: data analyst posts a 1-page summary note in Notion (or equivalent) on the last Monday of the month: key metrics, notable changes, one open question for the team to consider
Cadence: monthly
Facilitation: data analyst owns this
Why: addresses analyst's ambiguity about when to share proactively; creates a regular space that doesn't require them to interrupt anyone

---

Ritual ownership and maintenance plan:
- Weekly sync: rotating facilitator (all team members rotate after first month)
- Retro: PM facilitates first 3 months; hand off to team after that
- Monthly data share: data analyst owns; PM reviews before it's posted
- Ritual review: every 6 weeks in retro — is each ritual still delivering value?

---

Ritual effectiveness review process:
- After month 1: ask the team "which ritual feels most valuable, which feels like overhead?"
- Monthly: check meeting attendance and participation patterns
- If a ritual has <80% attendance for 3 consecutive weeks: either the time is wrong or the ritual isn't needed
